A graph view does two different things over time: it **reads** a corpus, and it **lays out** points.
`@kanzo-tech/graph` keeps them on two axes, runs the layout whenever the data does not say where a
point goes, and moves the camera for the reader only while a layout is moving the points.

## `status` and `motion` are two axes

| | Values | Moves when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `status` | `none` · `loading` · `idle` · `failed` | the graph, a binding or a filter is read and drawn, something fails |
| `motion` | `running` · `paused` · `settled` | a layout starts, is paused, or cools to rest |
| `progress` | `0` – `1`, in twentieths | a running layout cools toward rest |

**`status` is the data's life.** `none` is no catalog named; `loading` is the graph, a binding or a
filter not yet read or not yet drawn; `idle` is all of it drawn; `failed` is a corpus that would not
read, or a canvas with no GPU device — none came up, or its context was lost.

**`motion` is the layout's.** `running` is a live layout; `paused` is one waiting for the reader;
`settled` is one that converged on its own — or none ever ran, which is how a graph with bound `x`
and `y` loads.

**They are separate because each moves without the other.** A filter loads under a paused layout —
`loading` and `paused` — and a layout runs over a graph with nothing left to read — `idle` and
`running`. Folding them into one state would make one of those unsayable. So a word that sums them
reads both, and **`GraphStatus` is that word**: Loading while anything is read, Laying out with the
percentage while a layout runs over a drawn graph, Ready when both are at rest, Failed. A footer
draws the part rather than reading the axes:

```tsx
<GraphStatus />
```

Reading `status` alone is the mistake it exists to prevent: a footer that did said "idle" over a
layout visibly running. `GraphCounts`' `aria-busy` follows the load alone, because a layout moves the
points and not the figures it reports.

## When the layout runs

**Cosmograph's rule: the data places the points, or the layout does.** Bind `x` and `y` to two
numeric columns — `x="lon" y="lat"` — and every point is drawn at its row's values and nothing
simulates; a vertex whose table lacks either column is not drawn. There is no base map: lon/lat as
`x`/`y` is a scatter of positions, which is what Cosmograph does too. Leave them unbound — a fossil
corpus carries no positions — and the points start from a seeded Gaussian around the centre of the
layout's square, its spread growing with the graph, and the GPU force simulation runs as soon as the
graph is drawn. The seed is the vertex count, so the same corpus starts the same way.

`cluster` names a column the running layout pulls together by — cosmos.gl's cluster force over
`setPointClusters`. It is a binding like any other: change it and the layout reheats.

**Latitude as `y` draws north up.** cosmos.gl's space is y-up, and the data's extent is placed in its
square by a positive scale and a translation, never a flip. A reader picks among the three —
the layout, two columns, the layout with a cluster column — through
[`GraphPlacement`](/docs/graph#placement), whose column selects offer the corpus's own fields.

`simulate` overrides the rule: `false` keeps an unbound graph at its seeded start, `true` runs a
layout over bound positions. **The toolbar's transport** runs and stops the layout whatever it says —
*Pause the layout* while running, *Resume the layout* when paused, *Run the layout* when settled,
and *Re-run the layout*, which releases every pin and starts again from full heat. The api is the
same transport: `resume()`, `pause()`, `restart()`. Changing `sim` while a layout runs reheats it, so a
reader tuning gravity sees the change; a paused layout stays paused.

## How the layout scales

Three settings follow the vertex count, each decided by measurement — cosmos.gl 3.4.0, an Apple M4
Pro, Chrome 154 headed, a real GPU, a community graph of four links per vertex. **The shape is done
in one to three seconds at every size**; what the settings buy is a layout that stops spending frames
soon after, at a frame rate a reader can pan through.

| Setting | Up to 200,000 vertices | Above |
| --- | --- | --- |
| how long it cools (`simulationDecay`, frames) | 600 | 240 |
| the layout's square (`spaceSize`) | 4,096 | 8,192 |
| links while it runs | drawn up to 250,000 links, hidden above — they return when it settles or pauses | the same |

| Vertices | Links | Decay | Square | Links while running | Load | Settled | Frame (median) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | 40,000 | 600 | 4,096 | drawn | 88 ms | 10.0 s | 16.7 ms |
| 50,000 | 200,000 | 600 | 4,096 | drawn | 156 ms | 12.3 s | 16.7 ms |
| 200,000 | 800,000 | 600 | 4,096 | drawn | 456 ms | 43.0 s | 66.7 ms |
| 200,000 | 800,000 | 600 | 4,096 | hidden | 457 ms | 11.9 s | 16.7 ms |
| 200,000 | 800,000 | 5,000 | 4,096 | drawn | 444 ms | unsettled at 120 s | 66.7 ms |
| 500,000 | 2,000,000 | 600 | 4,096 | hidden | 1,097 ms | 25.2 s | 33.7 ms |
| 500,000 | 2,000,000 | 240 | 8,192 | hidden | 1,128 ms | 11.0 s | 48.3 ms |

*Load* is `setPointPositions` plus `setLinks`, synchronous on the main thread. *Settled* is wall
time until cosmos.gl's alpha reaches its floor. The last row's graph has tighter communities (99% of
links inside one); on that graph the 4,096 square was still visually mixing at 20 s, and 8,192 was
settled by 3 s.

- **Links are the cost, not the forces.** At 800,000 links a layout ticked 14 times a second with
  links drawn and 51 without. With links drawn throughout, the practical limit is about 50,000
  vertices.
- **A longer decay buys nothing.** At 5,000 frames the 200,000-vertex layout looked done by 10 s and
  was still running at 120 s.
- **Greying needs both index lists.** `highlightedPointIndices` alone leaves links lit;
  `highlightedLinkIndices` is set beside it — [the filter](/docs/design/graph#the-graph-is-a-client-of-the-pages-coordinator).

Measured on 2026-10-02, one run per row.

## Drag to fix, release to reheat

In the reading posture — no tool armed — a drag on the background pans and a drag on a node moves it.
What happens when the node is dropped depends on whether a layout can move it back:

- **While a layout is running or paused, the node is pinned where it is dropped.** It is d3-force's
  drag-to-fix: the forces keep acting on everything else, and the pinned node holds. The toolbar
  then shows **Release N pinned nodes**, and `pinned` lists them.
- **With no layout running, a drag only moves the node.** A pin is a promise that the layout will not
  move it, and nothing is pulling against it; pinning there would leave pins that mean nothing.

**Releasing reheats.** `unpin()` — the Release button — lets go of every pin and puts heat back into
the layout, so the released nodes visibly flow back into place:

| The layout was | After Release |
| --- | --- |
| running | takes the heat and goes on |
| settled | runs, and cools to settled on its own |
| paused | runs for a bounded burst of 1.2 s, then pauses again — the reader paused it |

## Where the layout simulates

cosmos.gl simulates in a square, `[0, spaceSize]²`, and two of its forces care where the points are
in it: gravity pulls toward the square's centre, and the many-body force bins points on a grid over
the square. Drawing is translation-invariant; the layout is not. **An unbound graph is seeded in the
middle of the square above. Bound positions are centred in it, at their own scale**, so a layout
run over them does not contract into a corner; the extent is scaled down only past what the device
can simulate in — half its `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE`.

## The camera

**At load, the camera frames the bound positions' extent — or, for a layout, the seeded points — and
keeps it framed until you move it.** It
frames before anything is drawn, so the first frame is already the whole graph — and again when
cosmos.gl's device comes up and whenever the canvas changes size, because a canvas in a split panel
or beside a dock settles its width after mount, and a frame taken at the old size opens zoomed into
a corner.

**While a layout runs, the camera follows it.** A force layout contracts a spread-out graph, and a
camera that stayed put would watch it shrink to a dot. So every 900 ms the camera re-frames the
moving points, gliding over 700 ms, and frames them once more when the layout settles — Cosmograph's
fit-on-settle, kept up for the whole run.

**The reader can take the camera back.** A wheel, a pan or a pinch hands the camera to the reader:
a resize leaves it where it is, and a running layout stops following until the next run starts. Fit
hands it back. A camera that fought the
reader's zoom would be the bug, not the feature.

**Fit frames what is there.** Before any layout has run, Fit frames the bound extent. Once a
layout has moved the points the extent is history, so Fit frames where the points are now. `reveal`
zooms to one vertex and `frameSelection` frames what is selected; neither changes who holds the
camera.

**A frame of the points waits for the points.** Framing where the points are reads them back from
the GPU, so it waits for cosmos.gl's device and for the render that uploaded them — and the layout
starts only after that render, because cosmos.gl 3.4 turning its simulation on discards an upload
that has not been rendered yet. If the read fails anyway, the device holds no positions: the graph
fails once with `graph/no-positions` and the camera stops reading, rather than leaving a blank canvas
that throws on every tick.

## Large corpora

The camera that follows a layout is not free. **Every re-frame after a layout has run reads every
point's position back from the GPU** — a synchronous `readPixels` of the position texture, sixteen
bytes a point, and an array of two numbers a point on the JavaScript side. At a few thousand points
it is invisible; past tens of thousands it is a stall each time, and a running layout asks for one
every 900 ms on top of a simulation step that already costs 10 ms at 50,000 points and 62 ms at
200,000.

What to do about it, in the order it pays:

- **Bind `x` and `y`** when the data has positions: nothing runs and nothing is read back.
- **Take the camera** — a wheel or a pan — once a run has started, and the re-frames stop until the
  next run.
- **Start large corpora at other forces** through the tenant policy over `GRAPH_SECTION`'s axes — less
  repulsion, more friction — so a run settles in fewer frames.
