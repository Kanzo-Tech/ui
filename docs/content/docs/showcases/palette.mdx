White-label is a data problem here, not a deploy. A client supplies two colours — a brand and a
neutral — and everything they touch is derived from that pair once, measured against their own
surfaces, and stored as a **palette document**. `--primary`, the surfaces, the charts, the graph
and the editor all read the same artefact, because there is nowhere else to configure any of them.

## What the screen is for

The gate policy is **adjust and publish** — never accept-and-warn, never refuse. A brand red at
2.1:1 becomes a legal red of the same hue, and the record of that happening is what this screen
shows: the rule that forced the move, the colour before and after, the ΔE, and the signed OKLCH
components, because "darker, and slightly less saturated" is a very different thing to hear than
"a different colour".

Three facts about the record are worth knowing before you read one.

<Callout type="warn" title="Relief rows are the healthy state, and the ramp is what tells you which is which">
  `carries-identity` — a step 9 below the chroma floor of 0.1 — is the one obligation that can
  never become an adjustment. Below the floor there is no nearest legal value; the nearest
  chromatic colour would be a hue *we* chose. So it is published as **relief** rather than
  repaired.

  A tinted neutral is a low chroma at a hue **by definition**, so it reports this every single
  time. Every correct two-seed palette in the tabs above has relief rows, and Kanzo's own document
  has four and zero adjustments. On the **neutral** ramp it is the expected answer; on the
  **brand** ramp it is something to tell the client.

  Distinguish by **ramp**, never by presence. A screen that renders any relief as an alert makes
  every correct palette look broken.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Relief is not the fill rule, and Nord is why">
  The consequence people expect from brand relief — `--primary` moving off step 9 onto step 12,
  the ramp's ink — is **not** what this row triggers. That rule fires on a ramp with no hue at all
  (`hue === null`, the encoding-noise regime below chroma 0.0035), and `carries-identity` grades
  step 9 against a much higher bar, 0.1. Two of the ramp's three regimes therefore report it.

  Nord is the case that separates them. Its brand `#88c0d0` reaches chroma 0.063 at step 9 —
  under the floor, so the row is published — at hue 217.5°, which is unmistakably blue. It keeps
  step 9: `--primary` is `#6299a9` in light and `#679eae` in dark. Read the relief as the fill rule
  and it compiled to `#001016` and `#dcf1f7` instead, which is a client's blue thrown away.

  Kanzo's own `#737373` is the other case: no hue, so step 12, `#0f0f0f` and `#efefef`.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Only the lightness moves">
  Across all five shipped seed pairs, every adjustment is a lightness move: chroma shifts by at
  most 0.001 and hue by under 0.21°. That is the price the system charges — a foreign seed keeps
  its hue and gives up its place. Nord's brand pays 11.8 ΔE in light and 10.8 in dark, and every
  point of that is lightness.
</Callout>

## Authored, and derived

Dracula, Nord and the two Catppuccins ship as `PALETTE_SEEDS` and go through `derivePalette`
exactly as a client does. They are not a second shape — a base16 palette is a brand hue and a
neutral hue, and two shapes for one idea is the defect this layer exists to remove.

<PreviewIframe className="h-[900px]" name="palette-base16" source="palette-onboarding/base16" title="base16 seeds, authored beside derived" />

The **This page is wearing** control at the top is the runtime mechanism, whole: `compile(doc)` returns one
stylesheet with a `:root` block and a `.dark` block, and applying a tenant's identity is appending
it. There is no `data-palette`, no `data-accent`, no per-token override — all of those were ways to
express *part* of a palette at runtime, and a document expresses all of it at once.

The old rule — "a palette may be dark-only; do not invent a light side for Dracula" — was true
when a palette was a set of authored hexes. Under derivation it is not: Dracula's `#ff79c6` ships
as `#e562af` at step 9, moved 7.3 ΔE by `visible-fill`, with the hue held to 0.12°. Same hue,
different place. That is a reading, not an invention.

## Source

```text
docs/showcases/palette-onboarding/
├── derive.ts         — every shipped seed pair, through the real derivation, on the server
├── default.tsx       — the onboarding screen (server)
├── panel.tsx         — its client half
├── base16.tsx        — authored beside derived (server)
├── base16-panel.tsx  — its client half
└── parts.tsx         — the ramp strip, the adjustment row, the relief verdict
```

Both routes are **server** components, and that split is the point rather than an optimisation.
Deriving one document costs 0.2–4.0 s — the categorical search dominates — which is fine once at
onboarding and unacceptable in a browser. It runs at build time; the client halves receive a
projection of the finished document and never import `derivePalette`.

See [Theming](/docs/theming#colour-the-palette-document) for the role table, the elevation model
and the rest of the pipeline.
