White-label is a data problem here, not a deploy. A client supplies two colours — a brand and a
neutral — and everything they touch is derived from that pair once, measured against their own
surfaces, and stored as a **palette document**. `--primary`, the surfaces, the charts, the graph
and the editor all read the same artefact, because there is nowhere else to configure any of them.

## Deriving your own

The row at the top is the whole claim, exercisable: type two hex seeds, press **Derive**, and a sixth
tab appears with a document nobody authored — its ramps, its categorical set, its record, its
stylesheet. The five beside it are the same function run at build time.

It derives on the **server**, through an action, and that is the architecture rather than a detail.
The categorical search costs 0.2–1.6 s, and `@kanzo-tech/palette` is kept out of the browser graph by
a test that matches on text — so even `import type` fails there. Derivation is authoring-time; the
runtime only applies what it produced.

<Callout type="info" title="What it emits is what the system ingests">
  Under **What ships** there are two artefacts and the smaller one is the source. The stylesheet is
  what a page loads; the `derivePalette` call is what the system takes *in*, so pasting it back
  reproduces the document byte for byte.

  That round trip is the table stake we were missing. daisyUI's generator emits the same `@plugin`
  block its plugin consumes; Material re-imports its own JSON. This page used to copy compiled CSS —
  and CSS is not our input format. Our input is two colours.
</Callout>

## What the screen is for

The gate policy is **adjust and publish** — never accept-and-warn, never refuse. A brand red at
2.1:1 becomes a legal red of the same hue, and the record of that happening is what this screen
shows: the rule that forced the move, the colour before and after, the ΔE, and the signed OKLCH
components, because "darker, and slightly less saturated" is a very different thing to hear than
"a different colour".

Two of its rows are read wrong more often than they are read right — a relief row is the *healthy*
state, and it is not the fill rule — and both are explained where the role table is, in
[Theming](/docs/theming#reading-a-record).

## Wearing one

The five palettes above are not a gallery — they are what this docs site *publishes*, and you can put
any of them on. Open **Customize** and pick one under PALETTE: the whole site repaints, and it is
still that palette after a reload.

That is the runtime mechanism, whole, and it is two halves rather than one. The server reads the
cookie and inlines the chosen document before the first byte, which is what makes a reload
flash-free; the browser fetches the document and swaps the same `<style>` when you pick another,
which is what makes the switch immediate. Neither half is optional and neither is the other's
fallback.

There is no `data-palette`, no `data-accent` and no per-token override. All of those were ways to
express *part* of a palette at runtime, and a document expresses all of it at once. A palette is
served, never selected in the cascade — which is why every non-default document is compiled with
`elevate`, its `:root:root` blocks outranking `tokens.css` on specificity rather than on whichever
stylesheet the browser happened to see last.

<Callout type="info" title="Dracula cannot be an identity">
  A tenant publishes two kinds of colour choice, and they are not interchangeable. An **identity** is
  a brand seed sharing the tenant's neutral, statuses and syntax roles — which is what keeps a bank's
  retail blue and its private gold one product. A **palette** is a whole document, surfaces included.

  So Dracula-as-identity would be Dracula's pink over Kanzo's grey, which is not Dracula. Borrowed
  palettes are documents.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Relief is not the fill rule, and Nord is why">
  The consequence people expect from brand relief — `--primary` moving off step 9 onto step 12,
  the ramp's ink — is **not** what this row triggers. That rule fires on a ramp with no hue at all
  (`hue === null`, the encoding-noise regime below chroma 0.0035), and `carries-identity` grades
  step 9 against a much higher bar, 0.1. Two of the ramp's three regimes therefore report it.

  Nord is the case that separates them. Its brand `#88c0d0` reaches chroma 0.063 at step 9 —
  under the floor, so the row is published — at hue 217.5°, which is unmistakably blue. It keeps
  step 9: `--primary` is `#6299a9` in light and `#679eae` in dark. Read the relief as the fill rule
  and it compiled to `#001016` and `#dcf1f7` instead, which is a client's blue thrown away.

  Kanzo's own `#737373` is the other case: no hue, so step 12, `#0f0f0f` and `#efefef`.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Only the lightness moves">
  Across all five shipped seed pairs, every adjustment is a lightness move: chroma shifts by at
  most 0.001 and hue by under 0.21°. That is the price the system charges — a foreign seed keeps
  its hue and gives up its place. Nord's brand pays 11.8 ΔE in light and 10.8 in dark, and every
  point of that is lightness.
</Callout>

## Source

```text
docs/showcases/palette-onboarding/
├── derive.ts         — every shipped seed pair, through the real derivation, on the server
├── default.tsx       — the onboarding screen (server)
├── panel.tsx         — its client half
└── parts.tsx         — the ramp strip, the adjustment row, the relief verdict
```

The route is a **server** component, and that split is the point rather than an optimisation.
Deriving one document costs 0.2–4.0 s — the categorical search dominates — which is fine once at
onboarding and unacceptable in a browser. It runs at build time; the client half receives a
projection of the finished document and never imports `derivePalette`.

See [Theming](/docs/theming#colour-the-palette-document) for the role table, the elevation model
and the rest of the pipeline.
