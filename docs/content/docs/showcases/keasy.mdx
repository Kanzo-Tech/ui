keasy is Kanzo's product, and this is two of its screens rebuilt on the library: **Dashboard**,
behind a collapsing rail with Settings in the user menu, and **Discover**, where a graph is
explored, full screen. They are one showcase because they are one product: one frame, one tenant,
one viewer, and screens that link to each other.

The route is the hash, the way keasy's are paths: `#/` and `#/graphs/archive/discover`. A link — in
the rail, in a trail, on a screen — is all navigation needs.
`/view/showcases/keasy#/graphs/archive/discover` opens on Discover.

## The frame

The layout is the canonical shadcn model, and it is the whole reason a fixed rail
and a full-width header are mutually exclusive: `SidebarProvider` is the viewport frame, and every
screen draws its `ShellHeader` **inside** `SidebarInset`, to the inline end of the rail — never
spanning it. `SidebarInset` carries no landmark; each screen owns the page's single `<main>`.

The sidebar arrangements the library does not ship as composites are written out by hand — the
workspace switcher, the nav with its active-path and mobile-close policy, the user footer, the
breadcrumb trail. That hand-composition **is** the documentation: an arrangement does not have to
become a component, and here you can read the whole of it. The header is the same on every screen:
the trigger where there is a rail, the trail from Dashboard, the screen's own controls, then search
and notifications. On a phone the trail keeps its last two crumbs, because the parent is the way
back.

- <kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>B</kbd> collapses the rail to icons; the switcher and user rows fall back to
  bare avatars and the header does not shift under it.
- <kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> opens the `Command` palette; <kbd>P</kbd> opens the `Preferences`
  drawer and re-themes every screen live. It holds only what is app-wide: the graph's looks live in
  Discover's dock, beside the canvas they change.

## Dashboard

The screen the product opens on, not a component catalogue: something that summarises state (four
`StatRoot`s), something actionable (the board table, with selection driving an `ActionBar`) and
something navigable (a `Steps` checklist, an activity list of `Item`s, and an `inert`-gated preview
of a feature that is not shipped yet).

It exists because the pieces only prove themselves together. A stat looks fine in a 384px
box; whether four of them sit correctly under a page header, beside a table whose toolbar has to
survive the rail collapsing to icons, is a different question — and the one a consumer has. Tick
rows in the table and the `ActionBar` rises with the count; **Take the tour** runs a four-step
`Tour` over the rail, the KPI row and the table toolbar.

## Discover

The other shape a shell takes: no page, no scroll, a canvas that fills whatever is left. Discover
draws no rail at all, so the frame is the canvas's; its trail, **Dashboard › Discover**, is the way
back. Here the graph is the Amber Hall's
**archive** — 536 closed contracts, the 938 field reports filed against them, and
the members, beasts, tags and regions they share. 1,543 vertices, 4,280 edges, drawn on the GPU.

`ShellMain` holds the canvas full-bleed and a `Resizable` docks a `ShellAside` beside it, so the
inspector is a panel of a splitter rather than a floating overlay: the drag, the keyboard resize and
the ARIA all come from the machine. Two orthogonal switches, which is the IDE shape — the header
picks what the main region shows (**Graph** · **Sightings**), the footer strip's
[`ShellDockSwitcher`](/docs/layout/shell#the-dock-switcher) picks which inspector the dock holds
(Info · Ask · Orders · Settings) and collapses it when you click the active icon again, a state
`Tabs` cannot express.

### The reference composition

This is the composition a product copies. One `GraphRoot` sits outside
the shell, so the canvas, the inspector and every dock panel read one graph and one crossfilter. The
canvas, the toolbar, the legend, the counts in the footer, the search and the inspector are
[`@kanzo-tech/graph`](/docs/graph)'s parts, and the showcase writes none of them. What it writes is
the product's: the channels the graph wears, the hall's name in the inspector, and the
dock's panels — **Info** (the search and the inspector), **Ask** ([`@kanzo-tech/ai/data`](/docs/ai/data): `useAskChat` over a
recorded model, each answer a dashboard tile read for real under the page's filter, drawn in an
`AnswerCard` with *Filter the page to these rows*), **Orders** and **Settings** (`GraphSettings`: the graph's section,
`PreferencesSections namespace="graph"`, above the gestures). fossil's
`attach` attaches the graph as the catalog `archive`, and the graph and the
[analytics subpath](/docs/analytics/charts)'s panels read it through the page's one engine —
[Discovery workspace](/docs/graph/workspace) is the wiring.

The archive carries no positions, so the layout runs on the GPU when it loads; the toolbar pauses
and re-runs it. A look or a zoom costs no query. A lasso or a click publishes the reader's pick into
the page's crossfilter as one `dense_id` clause, and every panel follows; a panel's filter greys
out what it drops on the canvas.

The canvas authors no colour: it draws in the page theme's categorical scale, so a theme from the
[theme generator](/theme-generator) recolours it with everything else.

### The standing orders

The Orders panel holds the hall's own rule document — the same small language the board's copy is
written in, over on [The Guild](/docs/the-guild) — parsed, compiled to SQL, and read against the
archive:

```text
order "a contract is signed for" {
  when    contract
  require signed at least 1
  else    violation "No warden's name on it."
}
```

Each order becomes one `count(*) FILTER (WHERE …)`, and the whole report is one query. Focus an
order and the ids that break it are published into the crossfilter, so the canvas lights up exactly
those nodes. Drop your own `.orders` file on the panel and it reads that instead; anything it cannot
compile is named rather than dropped, because otherwise you cannot tell "in order" from "never
read".

### Sightings

The other main view is a [`Dashboard`](/docs/analytics/dashboard) over the world's own analytical
relation, one row per reported sighting — the `boghound` met in the bestiary and as a hub on the
canvas. Everything on it is one hand-written [spec](/docs/analytics/dashboard-spec) and the
relation: a filter row, four tiles trending along the hour, a line, bars by hall coloured by the
verdict's status scale, bounty by region split into panels, and a fit of distance against bounty
over the rows, and the beasts as bars. The spec is written by hand because a showcase should look chosen; drop `value` and
the same component draws the automatic dashboard from the relation's stats. Edit any card and the
spec in state is what changed — the whole of what a host would persist. The editor draws in the
page's Format aside at the body's end edge, shown while a tile is edited, the way draw.io's Format
panel is.

`sightings` has no date column, so the ordered axis is `hour`, and each tile's delta compares an
hour with the one before. The view has its own `MosaicProvider`: it reads a different relation from
the canvas, and [a part says which relation it reads](/docs/analytics/pitfalls#a-part-of-your-own-on-more-than-one-relation).
It is nested in the graph's, and the page's slots are the outermost provider's, so the dashboard
reaches the `TileEditorAside` the shell places beside `ShellMain`.

Lasso a cluster and watch the toolbar count it. Find a contract in the Info search and the canvas
frames it while the inspector reads its row. Switch the Look in the dock's Settings; Ink moves identity from
colour to shape, and the legend's rows do not move. Focus an order. Then switch to **Sightings**: brush the hourly curve and every
panel narrows with it, click a hall's bar and the chip under the filters says so, and open a card's
editor to turn the line into an area.

## Source

```text
docs/showcases/keasy/
├── default.tsx           — the frame: the rail, the hash route, the tour and Preferences
├── header.tsx            — every screen's header: trigger, trail, search, notifications
├── command-palette.tsx   — the ⌘K palette behind the header's search button
├── data.tsx              — the routes, the nav, one hall's instances, KPIs and activity, all derived
├── dashboard.tsx         — Dashboard
├── board-table.tsx       — its table, built from the /table parts (selection, facets, view options)
├── discover.tsx          — Discover: header, splitter, footer strip, the canvas's parts placed
├── graph-view.tsx        — the GraphRoot composition, each look's channels, and the dock panels
├── graph-data.ts         — the archive as rows; what the graph is compiled FROM, not read from
├── corpus/               — the mapping, the shape and the script that compile it with fossil
├── duck.ts               — each view's relations, registered once in the page's one engine
├── orders.ts             — the standing-orders language, compiled to SQL predicates
├── order-builder.ts      — the same document, written back out
└── sightings-charts.tsx  — a `Dashboard` over the world's sightings, from a hand-written spec
```

Two subpaths beyond `@kanzo-tech/ui` and `@kanzo-tech/graph`: `@kanzo-tech/ui/table` for the board
and `@kanzo-tech/ui/analytics` for Discover. The Sightings
dashboard loads client-only, behind `ssr: false`, because evaluating vgplot during the RSC prerender
is a TDZ.
