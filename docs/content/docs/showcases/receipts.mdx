A photograph of five fuel tickets on a table becomes five rows of a ledger, and the ledger becomes
the CSV the accountant already uses. `ShellHeader` is the same dense utility strip
`metadata-form` uses — the source switch (**Recorded** · **Live model**), **Extract** and the
**CSV** download; `ShellMain` holds the editable table; a trailing `ShellAside` shows the ticket
the selected row was read from, above the findings against it, with **The shape** on the second
tab.

It exists to answer a question the other showcases do not reach: what does a screen look like when
the machine is *usually* right? Press **Extraer** and the rows fill in live. Six of the
thirty-five cells arrive empty, because six of them are not in the photograph — and that is the
screen, not a defect in the demo.

## One declaration, four readings

The seven columns are written once, as a SHACL node shape, and nothing below restates them:

```turtle
sh:property [
  sh:path tk:importe ;
  sh:name "IMPORTE"@es ;
  sh:description "El TOTAL en euros, el de «Total Venta». No el precio por litro de la columna €/L, ni el número de litros."@es ;
  sh:order 6 ;
  sh:datatype xsd:decimal ;
  sh:minCount 1 ;
  sh:message "El importe total del ticket, en euros."@es ;
] ;
```

None of that vocabulary is invented for this showcase. `sh:name`, `sh:description` and `sh:order`
are SHACL's **non-validating property shape characteristics** — in the Recommendation precisely so
a tool can present a shape rather than only validate against it. The one term this shape declines
is SHACL-UI's `shui:editor`: the control it would name is already implied by `sh:datatype` and
`sh:in`.

The strings are Spanish, tagged `@es`, while the chrome around them is English. That is the split,
not an oversight: `sh:name` becomes a column header in a Spanish accountant's spreadsheet and
`sh:message` is read by whoever checks a cell against Spanish paperwork.

[rudof](https://github.com/rudof-project/rudof) — a Rust SHACL/ShEx stack compiled to
WebAssembly — parses that document and hands back its own IR, so the column list *is* the shape.
The same document then decides:

1. **the table** — `sh:name` is the header, `sh:order` the column order, `sh:datatype` whether the
   cell is an `Input` or a `NativeSelect`, and `sh:in` the select's options;
2. **what the model is asked for** — the tool schema the route handler sends is generated from the
   projected columns, and `key` is an **enum of those columns**, so every cell comes back as an
   explicit `{key, value}` pair. Nothing is positional: the model never returns "the third value",
   and a key outside the enum is rejected by structured outputs. What tells it *which* is which is
   `sh:description` — the receipt prints a price per litre and a total side by side, and only that
   sentence says which one `importe` means;
3. **the findings** — `validate()` is the real SHACL validator running in wasm, not a lookalike;
4. **the file** — `toCsv` reads `sh:name` for the header and `sh:datatype` to know a date from a
   decimal.

Add a column to the shape and it appears in all four without a line changing anywhere else.

The converse is the part worth saying out loud: **the shape is a projection.** The receipt also
carries litres, €/L, the merchant, the concession, the operation number, the hour, the station and
the card holder — the shape names seven properties, so everything else is dropped and does not
exist downstream. That is what the accountant wants, and it is also why adding a column later
means re-reading the photographs.

Why that is a decision rather than a convenience is in
[`a-column-is-read-from-the-shape`](https://github.com/Kanzo-Tech/kanzo-ui/blob/main/decisions/a-column-is-read-from-the-shape.md).

## Why a model and not an OCR

Three of the seven columns are **not printed on the ticket at all**. `MATRÍCULA`, `DRIVER` and
`BASE/ESCOBA` are written on the paper in biro — a receipt OCR reads the printed layer and returns
nothing for them. Every low confidence in the recorded run is on one of those three, or on the one
ticket the photograph cuts in half.

That is also why the row thumbnail shows the *head* of each ticket rather than the whole of it:
the annotation is the part a reviewer needs to see.

## The review step is the screen

Extraction gets most cells right and some cells wrong, and two independent mechanisms say so:

- **the model's own note** — why a cell is empty or unsure, written for whoever has to check it
  against the paper (`Tapado por el portátil.`, `El ticket está cortado por el borde de la foto.`);
- **the shape** — `sh:minCount` turns a cell nobody could read into a violation, `sh:pattern`
  catches a mistyped ticket number, `sh:in` catches a base outside the vocabulary.

They agree without knowing about each other, and the aside shows both beside the crop of the
ticket. An unsure value carries a dotted amber underline; an invalid one gets `Input`'s own
`aria-invalid` ring.

## What it composes

Existing parts, arranged — nothing here is new:

- **`ShellRoot` / `ShellHeader` / `ShellBody` / `ShellMain` / `ShellAside`** — the frame. Exactly
  one `<main>`; the ticket panel is an `<aside>` landmark.
- **`useAiStream`** — the streaming engine, and its second consumer. It is domain-free by
  construction: a run of vision events uses it unchanged, alongside `Complete` and `Suggest`.
- **`FileUpload` + `FileUploadDropzone`** — the empty state. One photo may hold several tickets.
- **`Table` parts + `Input` + `NativeSelect`** — the editable ledger. The control a cell gets is
  decided by its `sh:datatype` and `sh:in`, not by a switch written here.
- **`DownloadTrigger`** — the CSV, `;`-separated with a BOM and the decimal comma left as typed,
  because that is the file Spanish Excel opens by double-click.
- **`SegmentGroup`**, **`Tabs`**, **`ScrollArea`**, **`Badge`**, **`Show`** — the chrome.

## Running it against a real model

The showcase ships a **recorded** run so the docs build is deterministic and needs no key. The same
`(shots, signal) => AsyncIterable<ExtractEvent>` seam takes a live adapter: set `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`
and **Live model** stops being greyed out. The showcase asks the route handler whether a key
exists before offering the option, rather than letting the button fail on click.

The key never reaches the browser, and `@kanzo-tech/ui` never learns what SHACL is — the admission
rule at the top of the barrel bars RDF by name, so every line of this lives in `showcases/`.
