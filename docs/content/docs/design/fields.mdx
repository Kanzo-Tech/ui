A dashboard, Ask, an answer's card and keasy's assistant each need to know two things about a
column: **what it is** — a number, a date, a year stored as an integer, an IRI, a key, a word in a
language, called *birth date* in English and *fecha de nacimiento* in Spanish — and **what it
holds** — how many rows, how many distinct values, how many missing, its range, its commonest
values. Today the corpus states the first exactly and the library throws it away on the way in, then
re-derives a cruder version of it from the column's name; and the second is read twice, by two
`SUMMARIZE`s that need not agree.

| Fact | `fossil.json` | The catalog views today | `@kanzo-tech/graph` | `JoinGraph` | What the library does instead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a type's, an edge's, a column's IRI | `iri` | `fossil_tables.iri`, `fossil_columns.iri` | not read | absent | nothing; words are split out of identifiers |
| an edge's label | `label` | absent | re-parsed from the table's name | `label` | a parse of `<Source>_<label>_<Destination>` |
| an edge's endpoint keys | `source.key`, `destination.key` | absent | not read | `src`, `dst` written as `"src"` and `"dst"` | a hard-coded pair |
| a literal's datatype | `datatype` | `datatype` | read | dropped | an `xsd:gYear` stored as an integer is a measure to average |
| a term's type | `term_type` | absent | not read | absent | an IRI column is guessed an identifier by its name or its cardinality |
| a column's role | `role` | `role` | read, to filter columns out | implicit | `ID_NAME` re-guesses identity by name |
| nullability | `nullable` | `is_nullable` | not read | absent | nothing |
| a multi-valued property | `property_tables` | `kind = 'property'` | skipped | absent | Ask cannot ask about one |
| what a column holds | absent | absent | absent | absent | a `SUMMARIZE` in `fieldStats`, another in `AnswerCard` |

## What a field is comes from the manifest

**Every fact the manifest states about a type, an edge or a column reaches `JoinGraph` unchanged,
and nothing downstream re-derives one.** The path is the one the data already travels: `fossil.json`
→ `@fossil-lang/corpus`'s catalog views → `@kanzo-tech/graph`'s structure reader → `JoinGraph` →
`@kanzo-tech/mosaic`, `@kanzo-tech/ui/analytics` and `@kanzo-tech/ai`. Two gaps are fossil's to
close, in the views: an edge's `label` and its endpoints' `key` columns, and a column's
`term_type` — the manifest has all three, the views drop them. A third is fossil's to add: the
annotations of `/docs/design/labels`, as their own view, a row per annotation. Then graph deletes its
two guesses — the label parsed out of a table's name, and `src`/`dst` written as constants.

**`JoinGraph` carries a column as a description, not a name.** A type's columns are each a column's
name, its DuckDB type, its `role`, its `datatype`, its `termType`, its `iri`, whether it is
`nullable`, its annotations, and what it holds (below); a type and an edge carry their own `iri` and
annotations. The key columns stay where they are, named by `key`, `src` and `dst`, now read and not
assumed. **One constructor builds a `JoinGraph` from a corpus, graph's**; keasy's second one, which
builds a graph from an accepted model with `id`, `src`, `dst` and its own table naming written in,
reads the corpus the model's dry run writes instead.

**The words are its labels.** A type's, an edge's and a column's words are its `rdfs:label`
annotation in the page's language, as [Tasks](/docs/design/tasks#words-come-from-the-datas-own-metadata)
says; fossil writes a SHACL shape's `sh:name` as `rdfs:label`, so a reader asks one predicate and
holds no preference list of its own.

The reference is the manifest itself — `/docs/format` on fossil's site, the contract a corpus
fulfils — and, for the vocabulary of a term, R2RML's: `rr:termType` (`rr:IRI`, `rr:BlankNode`,
`rr:Literal`) and the XSD datatypes, which is what fossil writes. A semantic layer reads the same
way: Looker's dimension `type` and Malloy's schema-read types come from the warehouse's declaration,
never from a column's spelling.

**What would reverse it:** a reader that needs a fact the manifest does not hold. Then it is added to
the manifest, by fossil, and travels the same path — never derived on the reader's side.

## What a field holds comes from fossil's profile, stored in the corpus

**fossil writes, beside every table of a corpus, what `fossil-profile` reads of it: the row count,
and per column the distinct count, the null count, the extent and the commonest values; the library
reads that and runs no `SUMMARIZE` over a corpus.** The profile already exists — it is how keasy's
assistant reads a source, `fossil-profile`'s plan run on the host's DuckDB — and running it over
what the executor wrote, at the end of a run, gives every reader the same figures the assistant was
given. Where it is written and in what shape is fossil's `/docs/format` to say; what the library
needs from it is this list, and that it is the profile's own vocabulary, so that one change on
fossil's side is one change here.

A relation that is not a corpus's — a host's own table under `MosaicProvider` — has no profile, and
is read with one `SUMMARIZE` through [the read door](/docs/design/reads), once and cached. That is
the only `SUMMARIZE` left, and it is never a second one: `AnswerCard` reads the facts the answer was
asked over, and the model and the card are told the same distinct count.

**Discarded: Parquet's own statistics.** Each row group's footer carries a minimum, a maximum and a
null count, read for free. It carries no distinct count and no commonest values, which are the two
figures a field's kind and Ask's prompt need, and a corpus of many row groups would still have to be
merged into one figure by somebody. *Back if* fossil's profile costs a run more than readers gain,
measured — then the extent and nulls come from the footers and the profile keeps only what they lack.

**What would reverse it:** a corpus written by something other than fossil, as the common case.
Then the profile is a reader's step, run once per corpus through the read door, and still not one per
component.

## A field's kind and role are read, not guessed

**A field's kind is Mosaic's `jsType` of its DuckDB type, unless its datatype says otherwise; its
role is a fact of the corpus, and where the corpus has none it is the kind's.** No `ID_NAME`, no
`DIMENSION_LIMIT`.

| Field | Decided by | Reads as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a column whose `role` is an identity, an address or an endpoint | the manifest | identifier |
| a column whose `termType` is an IRI or a blank node | the manifest | identifier |
| a column the profile finds a key — as many distinct values as rows, none missing | the profile | identifier |
| a literal whose datatype is a date or time — `xsd:date`, `xsd:dateTime`, `xsd:gYear`, `xsd:gYearMonth` | the manifest | temporal, a dimension |
| any other number | its DuckDB type | numeric, a measure |
| anything else | its DuckDB type | categorical, a dimension |

A relation with no corpus behind it has no role and no datatype, so the last two rows decide it: a
number is a measure, the rest a dimension, and nothing is an identifier because nothing said so. A
column of a joined relation is described by its step's type; its distinct count is the type's, which
is what a default needs, not the joined relation's.

**What it costs:** a string that is nearly but not quite unique per row, and that the shape does not
declare, reads as a dimension. A chart over it offers thousands of bars; the bar chart's own band
still cuts its labels, and the profile's distinct count is beside the field for a recommender to
read. Today the 50-value cut-off hides that case and misfiles every category of 51 values with it.

**What would reverse it:** dashboards defaulting, in readers' hands, to charts over columns the data
never declared and that are identifiers to anybody who reads them — then the remedy is in the shape
(a key, an IRI), and if no shape can say it, a rule with its evidence beside it, never a spelling.

## A multi-valued property is a relation

**A property table becomes a step of the join graph: a hop from its type, named by the property,
whose far side is the property table joined on the source's key.** A path that ends in it —
`name` on a laureate whose `name` is `Seq<LangString>` — reads its value column, and its language
column where it has one. It fans out like any hop, and `count` still counts the measured type
([Analytics](/docs/design/analytics#count-counts-the-measured-type)), so *laureates by name* counts a
laureate once per name and never twice per row.

The reference is Malloy's `join_many` and fossil's own first normal form: fossil keeps a
multi-valued property in a table of its own precisely so that a reader joins it, and today the
library's structure reader skips those tables, so no dashboard, picker or question can reach them.

**What would reverse it:** a property table that is not a function of its source — a value with no
source key — which fossil's format does not allow today.

**Breaking**, in a consumer's words:

- A `JoinGraph` type's `columns` are column descriptions, not names. If you build a `JoinGraph` by
  hand, build it from the corpus with `@kanzo-tech/graph` instead.
- A column named `id`, `uri` or `iri` is no longer an identifier because of its name. If a field
  your dashboard grouped by is now a measure or a dimension, declare it in the shape.
- `fieldStats` no longer reads `SUMMARIZE` rows over a corpus, and `queryFieldStats` is gone;
  `useFieldStats` reads the corpus's facts, or one `SUMMARIZE` through the read door for a table
  that is not a corpus's.
- `answerRelationsOf` and `readAnswerRelations` are gone: Ask is offered the graph's facts.
- A multi-valued property appears as a hop in `RelationPicker` and as a path in Ask.
- A corpus written before fossil writes its profile is rebuilt; the library does not read one
  without it.
