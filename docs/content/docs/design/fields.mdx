*Status: partly to be built.* The tree takes these facts from fossil's four manifest relations
(`fossil_tables`, `fossil_columns`, `fossil_annotations`, `fossil_stats`), through graph's
`readCatalog` and a `joinGraphOf` that renames its `Structure` into a `JoinGraph`. Decided: a
`CatalogReader` over an attached GraphAr graph makes the `JoinGraph`, `withMapping` fills the labels
and datatypes the catalog does not say, and the shape before a run is the compiler's
([relations](/docs/design/relations)). Where a source for a fact is not yet stated, the table says so
and the fact is absent until one does; nothing derives it. The *Breaking* list at the end is what a
consumer edits.

A dashboard, Ask, an answer's card and keasy's assistant each need to know two things about a
column: **what it is** — a number, a date, a year stored as an integer, an IRI, a key, called *birth
date* in English and *fecha de nacimiento* in Spanish — and **what it holds** — how many distinct
values, how many missing, its range, its commonest values. The graph states both: what each column is
is the catalog's and the mapping's, and what it holds is counted when the graph is written. The library used to throw the first away on the way in and re-derive a cruder version of it
from the column's name, and to read the second twice, by two `SUMMARIZE`s that need not agree, and a
count per category on top for Ask.

| Fact | Where the graph states it | `JoinGraph` | Before |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a type's, an edge's, a column's IRI | undecided: the mapping or GraphAr's extra information | `iri` | not read |
| an edge's label | `graphar_edge_types.edge_type` | `JoinEdge.label` | parsed out of `<Source>_<label>_<Destination>` |
| an edge's endpoint keys | the endpoint indices, reconciled with each type's `first` by the reader | `src`, `dst` | written as `"src"` and `"dst"` |
| a literal's datatype | the mapping, through `withMapping` | `JoinColumn.datatype` | dropped: an `xsd:gYear` stored as an integer was a measure to average |
| a term's type | undecided: the mapping or GraphAr's extra information | `JoinColumn.termType` | an IRI column guessed an identifier by its name or its cardinality |
| a column's role | `address` is the reader's, from the vertex index; the rest undecided | `JoinColumn.role` | `ID_NAME` re-guessed identity by name |
| nullability | `graphar_properties.is_nullable` | `JoinColumn.nullable` | not read |
| a label in a language | the mapping, through `withMapping` | `annotations` | words split out of identifiers |
| what a column holds | extent and nulls: the chunks' Parquet footers; distinct count and commonest values: undecided | `JoinColumn.stats` | a `SUMMARIZE` in `fieldStats`, another in `AnswerCard`, a count per category for Ask |
| the keys the writer found | `graphar_properties.is_primary` is a declared key; a discovered candidate has no place | `JoinType.candidateKeys` | a 50-value cut-off |
| a multi-valued property | GraphAr's `cardinality`, read by the reader as a type of its own | a type with a `value`, and the hop to it | skipped |

## What a field is comes from the manifest, through one read

**Every fact the catalog or the mapping states about a type, an edge or a column reaches `JoinGraph`
unchanged, and nothing downstream re-derives one.** The path is one: the attached GraphAr graph →
`@kanzo-tech/relations`'s `CatalogReader`, the only way a `JoinGraph` is made from an engine →
`withMapping`, which fills labels and datatypes from the program's mapping and adds no type and no
column → `@kanzo-tech/ui/analytics`, `@kanzo-tech/ai/data` and `@kanzo-tech/graph`. A host with no
graph yet gets the same model from the compiler's `describe` over the declared types
([relations](/docs/design/relations#the-pre-run-shape-comes-from-the-compiler)). A column's DuckDB
type is read in the same breath from the engine's own `duckdb_columns()`: what the engine will read
the view as, never a mapping of fossil's spelling kept here.

**`JoinGraph` carries a column as a description, not a name** — `JoinColumn`: its name, its DuckDB
type, and whatever the catalog states of it, `role`, `datatype`, `termType`, `iri`, `nullable`,
`annotations` and `stats`. A type and an edge carry their own `iri` and annotations, a type its
`candidateKeys`. Only `name` and `type` are required, so a host's own graph describes what it knows —
the dashboard example on `/docs/analytics/dashboard` declares its table's key that way.
`relationColumns(graph, relation)` describes a relation's columns under the names it reads them by:
a column of a joined relation is described by its step's type, so its counts are the type's, which
is what a default needs, and not the join's.

**Every catalog read is behind a `CatalogReader`**, so a change in how a graph states itself is a
change in one function, and the model it answers does not move
([The graph](/docs/design/graph#one-catalog-type-and-the-graph-keeps-none)). fossil's graph moves to
Apache GraphAr, and fossil deletes `attach` and the `fossil_*` relations with it; the reader of a
relation nothing makes is not kept.

The reference is the catalog's own specification — Apache GraphAr's format, which numbers vertices per
type and is the reader's to reconcile — and, for the vocabulary of a term, R2RML's: `rr:termType` and
the XSD datatypes, which is what a mapping states. A semantic layer reads the same way: Looker's dimension `type` and Malloy's
schema-read types come from the warehouse's declaration, never from a column's spelling.

**Three facts have no source yet, and the page says so rather than choose one:** a term's type, an IRI
and a column's role beyond the key. GraphAr's standard states neither, the mapping may, and GraphAr's
extra information may; which is fossil's migration to settle. Until a source states them, they are
absent from the `JoinGraph`, a column with no role reads by its DuckDB type, and an IRI column is an
identifier only if something said so. This is the rule working, not a gap in it.

**What would reverse it:** a reader that needs a fact neither the catalog nor the mapping holds. Then
it is added to one of them, by fossil, and travels the same path — never derived on the reader's side.

*Not yet held:* a test that reads a vendored GraphAr fixture through the real attacher and the real
reader and asserts every row of the table above, and a `layers.test.ts` case that no shipped source
names a catalog relation of the old format. The fossil-relation tests this replaces are deleted with
the reader.

## What a field holds comes from counts stated in the graph

**A graph states, for every column of every table, the counts a field needs — its nulls, its distinct
values, its extent and its commonest values with their share of the rows — under Parquet's
`Statistics` names and `pg_stats`', and the library reads those and runs no `SUMMARIZE` over a
counted graph.**
`useFieldStats` over a relation of the page's joins whose columns are all counted queries nothing;
Ask is offered each type's fields off the graph, its commonest values the writer's; `AnswerCard`
draws by the facts the answer was asked over, so the model and the card are told the same figures.
`JoinColumnStats` keeps the reference's names, camel-cased, so a change on fossil's side is a change
of one name here.

A relation whose columns nothing counted — a host's own table under `MosaicProvider`, or the
`vertices` union, whose tables were counted one by one but never together — is read with one
`SUMMARIZE` through [the read door](/docs/design/reads), once and cached, and what the joins say each
column *is* is kept over the summary's counts. That is the only `SUMMARIZE` left.

**Decided differently from the plan:** the plan had the library refuse a graph written before the
writer counted. It reads one — its catalog answers a row of nulls per column — and such a graph is a
relation nothing counted: one `SUMMARIZE` for a dashboard, and Ask offered its fields without values.
Refusing it would have been a failure for a reader to see where a slower read does.

**Parquet's own statistics are what a GraphAr graph gives for free, and no more.** Each row group's
footer carries a minimum, a maximum and a null count, read without scanning the data, and the reader
merges the row groups into one figure per column. It carries no commonest values and, in practice, no
distinct count, which are the two figures a field's role and Ask's prompt need. So the extent and the
nulls come from the footers, and where the distinct count and the commonest values are to be stated —
by the writer in the graph's extra information, or read once per relation — is undecided and fossil's
to settle with its migration. Until it is, a relation whose columns lack them is the relation nothing
counted, which the paragraph above already designs: one `SUMMARIZE`, once, cached.

**What would reverse it:** a graph whose writer states no counts, as the common case. Then
the counts are a reader's step, run once per graph through the read door, and still not one per
component.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/charts/field-stats.test.tsx`, "reads nothing for a relation over the page's
joins whose every column the corpus counted"; "summarizes a relation over the joins the corpus did not
count, keeping what the graph says each column is"; `packages/ai/src/data/answer.test.ts`, "reads
nothing from the engine: what a field is and holds is the graph's"; `packages/ai/src/data/answer-card.test.tsx`,
"draws an answered tile at once, never busy: its fields are the graph's, and nothing is read for them".

## A field's kind and role are read, not guessed

**A field's kind is Mosaic's `jsType` of its DuckDB type, unless its datatype makes it a time; its
role is what the data declares, and where it declares nothing, the kind's.** No `ID_NAME`, no
`DIMENSION_LIMIT`.

| Field | Decided by | Reads as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a column whose `role` is `address`, `identity` or `endpoint` | the manifest | identifier |
| a column whose `termType` is an IRI or a blank node | the manifest | identifier |
| a column among its type's `candidateKeys` — never null, never repeated | the writer's count | identifier |
| a number or a time whose datatype is `xsd:date`, `xsd:dateTime`, `xsd:gYear` or `xsd:gYearMonth` | the manifest | temporal, a dimension |
| any other number | its DuckDB type | numeric, a measure |
| anything else | its DuckDB type | categorical, a dimension |

A year stored as its integer is a time with its extent as the years' first days, and a trend over it
bins the number it is. A relation with no graph behind it has no role and no datatype, so the last
two rows decide it, and nothing is an identifier because nothing said so; a host says it in the
joins it hands the provider.

**What it costs:** a text nearly but not quite unique per row, which the shape does not declare,
reads as a dimension — the dashboard offers a facet filter over thousands of values, and a chart over
it thousands of bars, which the bar chart's own band still cuts. The 50-value cut-off hid that case
and misfiled every category of 51 values with it.

**What would reverse it:** dashboards defaulting, in readers' hands, to charts over columns the data
never declared and that are identifiers to anybody who reads them — then the remedy is in the shape (a
key, an IRI), and if no shape can say it, a rule with its evidence beside it, never a spelling.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/charts/field-stats.test.tsx`, "reads a kind from Mosaic's type words, and
a role from what the data says and nothing else"; "reads a time by its datatype, a year stored as its
integer among them, and its extent as the years' first days".

## A multi-valued property is a step

**A property table is a type of its own — a row per value, with a `value`, the column a path that
ends on it reads — reached from its source by a hop named by the property, through the table itself.**
The key is held in the row, so the hop is one join, not two: an edge whose table is the table of the
type it reaches is a key held there, which is what a property table is. A path that ends on it,
`laureate.nickname`, reads its value, never `laureate.nickname.nickname`; its language column reads
beside it. It fans out like any hop, and `count` still counts the measured type
([Analytics](/docs/design/analytics)), so *laureates by nickname* counts a laureate once per nickname
and never twice per row. Ask writes such a path, and never measures a property on its own.

The reference is Malloy's `join_many` and fossil's own first normal form: fossil keeps a
multi-valued property in a table of its own precisely so that a reader joins it, and the structure
reader skipped those tables, so no dashboard, picker or question could reach them.

**What would reverse it:** a property table that is not a function of its source — a value with no
source key — which fossil's format does not allow.

*Held by* `packages/mosaic/src/crossfilter.test.ts`, "joins its table once, on the key it holds, so a
laureate's row is one per nickname and never one per pair"; "resolves a path that ends on it to the
step, and reads its value there"; `packages/graph/src/core/join-graph.test.ts`, "reads a multi-valued
property as a type of its own, reached by a hop named by the property through its own table";
`packages/ai/src/data/ask.test.ts`, "is a path Ask may write and read its value by, and never a type it
measures".

## The words are its labels

**A type's, an edge's and a column's words are its `rdfs:label` in the page's language** —
`<html lang>`, by RFC 4647's lookup, then an untagged literal — `labelOf`. fossil writes a SHACL
shape's `sh:name` as `rdfs:label` and its `sh:description` as `rdfs:comment`, so a reader asks one
predicate and holds no preference list of its own. A type's naming column is the one whose property
IRI is a labelling property — `skos:prefLabel`, `rdfs:label`, then schema.org's and FOAF's `name`,
in rdflib's order — `namingColumn`, and it reads as its type: *university.name* is *university*.
`relationColumns` gives each column the label the data gives it, `fieldStats` carries it as
`FieldStat.label`, and a tile's title, Ask's chips and `RelationPicker`'s sentences read it
([Tasks](/docs/design/tasks)).

**Decided differently from the plan:** where nothing labels a field, a title reads its path, not the
identifier split — a path is what the reader edits in the tile's field pickers, and the two would
read differently. The split, `splitWords`, is the picker's last resort for a type or an edge, and it
never inflects. Two paths of one length that reach one field read the same words, so the chip offers
the other by its path.

**What would reverse it:** an evaluation set on which readers misread an answer's labelled words —
then a host's own labels, handed in, win over the data's, and still no word is the code's.

*Held by* `packages/mosaic/src/words.test.ts`, "reads the label in the page's language by RFC 4647's
lookup, then an untagged one"; "is the column whose property labels, preferred as rdflib prefers,
never one because it is called name"; "are its own label, or its type's for the type's naming column,
and absent where nothing labels it".

## What it changed for a consumer

- A `JoinGraph` type's `columns` are `JoinColumn` descriptions, not names. Build one from a graph
  with `@kanzo-tech/relations`' reader, and fill its labels and datatypes with `withMapping`; a graph
  of your own describes at least each column's `name` and `type`.
- A term's type, an IRI and a column's role beyond the key are absent until the catalog or the
  mapping states them; nothing in the library guesses them.
- A column named `id`, `uri` or `iri` is no longer an identifier because of its name, and a text of
  more than 50 values is no longer one because of its count. Declare a key in the shape, or in the
  joins you hand the provider.
- `useFieldStats` takes the relation as `ChartRoot` does, reads a counted graph's facts, and runs one
  `SUMMARIZE` only for a relation nothing counted. `fieldStats` takes column descriptions;
  `SummarizeRow` is gone.
- `answerRelationsOf`, `readAnswerRelations`, `AnswerField`, `AskInput.types` and
  `AskChatOptions.types` are gone: Ask is offered the graph's facts.
- A multi-valued property is a hop in `RelationPicker` and a path in Ask.
- A tile's title, Ask's chips and `RelationPicker` read the data's labels.
