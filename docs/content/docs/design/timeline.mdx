A timeline is a brushable histogram of one temporal column, which filters the page to the brushed
range and can play that range forward. `ChartTimeline` is the analytics part; `GraphTimeline` places
one under a graph canvas over the corpus's temporal columns. The reference is Cosmograph's
`CosmographTimeline`, read from `@cosmograph/ui` and `@cosmograph/cosmograph` 2.5.1. This page is
why ours is shaped as it is.

## A histogram whose brush owns one clause

**A timeline is three layers of bars over `bin(field)` and an interval brush that publishes one
clause: the bars behind are the column unfiltered, grey; the two above are what the page keeps, the
window excluded — the range dimmed, and in front, in colour, what the clause holds — and the
brush's clause is a chip in the bar like any other control's.** The window is in colour and what it
leaves out is grey, as in Cosmograph: the layers above are clipped, so inside the window they cover
the grey bars behind and outside it only the grey shows. While play runs inside the range, the
clause holds less than the range, and the part of the range it has not reached is dimmed. The brush exempts
its whole plot, so a drag asks the database nothing here. A first version filtered the bars in front
by the window (`peers: false`) instead, and every step of a drag re-queried them; vgplot's `Plot`
redraws its whole svg for one mark's answer, brush included, so the timeline flickered and rebuilt
under the pointer. A clip is an attribute, drawn in the same frame. The brush is an outline,
because d3's grey fill over the window read as the part left out. *What would reverse it:* a Plot
that redraws one mark in place; then the bars in front can be the window's own query again, and
show its counts rather than its extent. Cosmograph's `Timeline` draws the same two
layers (`highlightSelectedData`) and publishes `isBetween(column, range)` from a client of its own.
Ours is a `ChartRoot` of `ChartRectY` marks and a `ChartBrushX`. The bins are SQL, through vgplot's
`bin()`, which bins a temporal channel by date.

**One clause means one source, for as long as the window lasts.** An interval is its clause's source,
and vgplot builds new interactors with every plot, so a plot rebuilt under a window left that window
in the selection with no brush to move or clear it, and the next brush published beside it: the
bar held two windows and intersected them. Two things rebuilt it. `bin()` returns a new closure per
call, which the plot's signature reads by identity, so every render was a new grammar: `bin` on
`/analytics` is made once per field and options. And a resize or a theme change rebuilds by design,
so **the clause's source is the interactor's place in the plot, not the interactor**: `ChartRoot`
gives each place a source for the root's life, and whichever interactor holds the place publishes
under it. Handing the clause from each interactor to the next was not enough. In keasy, the readout
beside the bars changed width as it played, every width a rebuild, and a rebuild that landed before
the last had drawn handed over from an interactor that had never published; a drag begun on the old
plot went on publishing through its brush, which d3 keeps listening to. The bar held four windows at
once. With one source per place the selection holds one clause whoever publishes, and an interactor
no longer on screen speaks through the one that is. The heir is handed the value, draws its brush
from it and republishes it for its own marks; where the grammar changed, the clause is withdrawn.
And the range is no longer drawn beside the bars, so it no longer resizes the plot. *What would reverse it:*
interactors in vgplot that outlive their plot, or a plot that resizes without being rebuilt; then
there is nothing to hand over and no place to keep.

**It replaced the dashboard's private `Timeline`**, the filter control behind a temporal field's chip
(`packages/ui/src/charts/dashboard-filters.tsx`), which drew the same marks with no playback and
could not be placed anywhere else. The chip renders `ChartTimeline` now: one implementation, as the
graph and the dashboard need the same part. **Cosmograph's binning was turned down**: it reads the
whole column into the browser and bins it in JavaScript, 250 bars by default. Ours asks the
coordinator for one row per bin, which a pre-aggregate can answer.

**What would reverse it:** a temporal column whose bins cannot be a `GROUP BY` — a time with a
timezone per row, read as wall-clock time. Then the bins are computed over the rows the client holds,
as Cosmograph's are.

## Play runs inside the brushed range, accumulating

**The brushed range is the reader's and never moves; play runs inside it.** Each step publishes
*[the range's start, one bar further]*, from the range's first bar to its end, so the graph fills
in over time. That is Kepler.gl's `incremental` animation window, whose start stays put while its
end grows to the end of the last bin
([`animation-controller.ts`](https://github.com/keplergl/kepler.gl/blob/f999fa696c202c5903c6ae2c9450cce83294a04b/src/components/src/common/animation-control/animation-controller.ts)
L214-232). It is also the reading of Gapminder's play, where the chart builds through time
([Vizabi `time.js`](https://github.com/vizabi/vizabi/blob/44bffd91a542b369321ee1ebcc882bc905b3537b/src/models/time.js)).
The brush stays drawn as the range's outline, the bars from its start to the frame are in colour,
the rest of the range is dimmed, and outside it is grey. A pause or the end shows the same, frozen.
When nothing plays, the range is a plain window.

**Cosmograph's moving window was built first and turned down.** Cosmograph's `Timeline` moves the
selection itself a bar at a time, keeping its width, and clears it at the end
(`@cosmograph/ui` 2.5.1, `modules/timeline/component.js`: `_animateSelection` and
`stopAnimation`). Built that way here, it threw away the range the reader had chosen: the window left
the years it was brushed over, and a sweep from a narrow window over a large graph greyed almost
everything. A reader who brushes 1980 – 1990 is asking to watch those years, so the range stays and
the frame grows inside it. *What would reverse it:* readers who want a fixed-width window moving over
time — a rolling year; then a second mode, Kepler's `free`, beside this one, chosen in the settings.

**With no range, Play is disabled, as Cosmograph's is.** It is described, and its tooltip says why:
*Brush a range to play*. Cosmograph disables its control without a selection
(`_initAnimationControls` and the `disabled` class). The sweep from the axis's start that we tried
instead played a range nobody chose.

**At the end, it stops and keeps the whole range; the next Play starts over from its first bar.**
That is Vizabi's rule, *go to start if we start from end point*, and what Observable's
[Scrubber](https://observablehq.com/@mbostock/scrubber) does on the next press. Cosmograph clears the
selection at the end, which reads as play undoing the reader's range, and Kepler loops forever, which
never lets a graph settle. *What would reverse it:* a reader who wants it to loop; then a loop
setting, off by default.

**A pointer coming down on the bars or the brush while it plays pauses it.** Scrubber stops on any
input it did not make, Vizabi sets `playing = false` on a drag, and
[Animated Vega-Lite](https://vis.mit.edu/pubs/animated-vega-lite) pauses when the slider is scrubbed.
A drag makes a new range, and the next Play starts from that range's start. Cosmograph and Flourish
play on under the pointer, so the reader's gesture and the step fight over the same brush.

**Each step waits for the page to answer the last, and takes no less than
max(50 ms, 50 ms × 60 / bars).** The step publishes, then waits for the clients of the page
(`Selection.pending("value")`, which resolves once the coordinator has answered every client the
value reached, the graph's among them) and for that least time. Cosmograph's 50 ms a bar does not
wait, and on SNB's 341,661 vertices the canvas fell behind the readout. Kepler schedules its next
frame only after React has drawn the last, and Vizabi skips frames when they lag: both let the render
set the pace. A graph that answers slowly plays slowly, rather than out of step, and dashboards follow
at the same rate, each query a Mosaic client throttles to one in flight. On fewer bars a step takes
longer, so a sweep of the whole axis lasts about as long.

**Under `prefers-reduced-motion` a step takes 500 ms at least.** The rule on
[preferences](/docs/design/preferences) is to honour the query wherever something moves. Play is
what the person asked for and pause is one press away, so it still plays, ten times slower, each
frame a state to read. None of the references handles the query. *What would reverse it:* a reader
under the query for whom even that is too much; then Play steps one bar per press.

**One clause, and one chip, for the timeline's life.** Each frame is published through the
interval's own clause, whose source is its place in the plot (above), so a frame, a drag, a stuck
drag-end and a rebuild all replace the same clause. Cosmograph does the same with one
`FilteringClient` per timeline publishing as `source: this`. Mosaic depends on it: a crossfilter
selection drops queued values whose clause has the same source (`queueFilter`), so with one source a
burst of steps collapses to the newest. While it plays the clause says so in its metadata, and its
chip carries a ▶; its × stops play and lets the range go, as the page's **Clear** does.

**The timeline is a compact band, 44 px, drawn as Cosmograph's app draws it:** no border or panel of
its own, the tick labels in a row at the top, each to the right of a thin rule that runs the band's
height, and the bars under them. Plot's axis attributes cannot put a label beside its tick, so the
axis is Plot's `axisX` mark with `anchor: "top"`, `textAnchor: "start"`, and `gridX` for the rules
through the bars. The last tick, at the axis's end, has no room to its right and goes unlabelled.
With no range the bars are a low-contrast fill, the dimmed layer alone; a range is bright inside
it. *What would reverse it:* a timeline tall enough for a y axis, or labels that need the room a
bottom axis gives; then the axis returns under the bars.

**Play is a bare glyph at the band's left edge, muted until hovered, as Cosmograph's is.** Cosmograph
draws a borderless icon, 20 – 30 px wide and half opaque, as a flex item left of its bars
(`style.js`, `animationControl`). Ours is a solid 12 px glyph flush at the band's left, 4 px from the
bars, with no box at all: no border, background or hover tint. It sits muted at 0.6 opacity, rises to
the foreground at 1 on hover and focus, and to 0.2 when there is nothing to play. Keyboard focus
outlines the glyph alone. It is a plain `button`, not `Button`, whose recipe is the box. It is named *Play time* or *Pause time*, the same words as its
tooltip, so it does not read as the canvas toolbar's layout transport. The bars take every pixel
right of it. The window's range is not drawn beside them: the chip reads it, and a slot that changed
width resized and so rebuilt the plot. It is still read, as a group whose `aria-valuetext` is the
window. A pause and the end are announced; the steps are not, since one every 50 ms would flood a
screen reader. Space on the focused figure plays and pauses.

**A clause belongs to the source that published it, and only that source changes it, so play asks
the brush's clause for each frame.** `interval.clause(frame)` builds it, in data rather than pixels:
`publish` inverts a pixel floored to the interactor's `pixelSize`, so a frame it set would hold
*1909.9* where the bar starts at *1910*.

**The range sticks to the bars' edges, Cosmograph's `stickySelection`.** When a drag ends — a
d3-brush `end` with a `sourceEvent`, which a move made in code never has — the range is moved to the
nearest edges of the bars the brushed mark drew, at least one bar wide. So it reads *1910 – 1940*,
its chip too, and each frame ends on an edge: one bar, whatever its width, a month of uneven days
included. The edges are the mark's own `x1` and `x2`, the bins vgplot asked for, so nothing is binned
twice. Mosaic draws this line itself. An interval interactor never follows its selection, while an
input that is driven from outside is bound to a `Param`: `Slider` follows a `Param`'s `value` events
and not a `Selection`'s. `ChartTimeline` reaches its interactor through `Plot.interactors`, which
vgplot exposes.

**Two alternatives were turned down.**

- **`value` and `onValueChange` on the brush**, Ark's controlled pair. React would hold the range
  beside the clause that already holds it: two sources of truth for one filter.
- **Patching mosaic-plot so an interval follows its selection.** That moves the brush when another
  source updates the clause, and the model says no other source does. Clearing is the one case where
  something else touches the clause: the bar's chip and the page's **Clear** retract it, and the
  interactor's `reset` already answers that.

**What would reverse it:** an interval interactor in Mosaic bound to a `Param` — the brush following
a value, as `Slider` does. Then play writes the `Param`, and `ChartTimeline` stops reaching into
`Plot.interactors`.

## Time is read from the schema, never guessed

**A column is temporal when the corpus says it is: a `date` or `timestamp` column, or one whose
`datatype` is `xsd:date`, `xsd:dateTime` or `xsd:gYear`.** `fossil_columns.datatype` carries the IRI
the shape declared, so an `xsd:gYear` stored as an `int32` is drawn as years. Cosmograph instead
samples 100 values and calls a column of year-like integers a year. A count of rows, a price or an
id in that range would be read as a date, and the schema already says which column is a year.

**The column is a setting of the graph, not a selector of the part's.** `time-by` sits beside
*X axis* and *Group by* in `GRAPH_SECTION`, and the root answers its options from the corpus's
temporal columns, as it answers theirs. Cosmograph's own app configures its timeline the same way, in
its settings panel. A selector inside the part would have been a second door to a choice the graph
already makes in one place. None, the default, draws no timeline. The histogram is the union of the
tables that have the column, one colour, and while a window is set only the vertices with the
column inside it stay in colour.

**The window reaches the page as identity, not as its column.** A window is `date BETWEEN …`, and
only the types with a date can answer it: published straight into the page's crossfilter, it reached
every client of the page, and a dashboard's relation or a filter over a type with no date failed on
the column it does not have. So the timeline publishes into a crossfilter of its own, bridged to the
page by `semiJoinOf(key, timeline)` — `dense_id IN (SELECT dense_id FROM <the types with the
column> WHERE <the window>)` — the way a dashboard's tiles cross. It names the key alone, so every
client answers it, without a Binder error. The chip reads *date 1910 – 1940*: the bar names a
bridged clause by its parts, and does not prefix a part with its bridge's name when the part already
reads as it — the bridge is called by the column.

**A vertex without the column is outside every window, so it is greyed, as Cosmograph greys it.**
The bridge was first an anti-join, `dense_id NOT IN (… WHERE NOT <the window>)`, which dropped only
the dated rows the window leaves out and kept every type without the column whole. In keasy that read
wrong: on SNB, a window over 30 birthdays left *340,163 of 341,661* vertices in colour, every post,
place and tag among them, and it looked like a window that selected more than it should. Cosmograph's
`Timeline` filters its points by `isBetween(column, range)`, and a point with no time answers false,
so it is greyed with what the window leaves out. The semi-join is that rule over identity: only the
rows that have the column and fall in the window are kept, so the same window reads *30 of 341,661*.
The edges follow by the rule that a link needs both its ends.

**A dashboard over a relation whose root has no time column shows no rows while a window is set.**
It answers the same clause through its root, and none of its keys are in the window. That is Mosaic's
model: a client applies every clause it is handed, and a cross-filter is the rows every clause keeps,
so a relation that cannot be in the window is not shown as if it were. It is not left whole, which
would say the window had nothing to do with it. Why it is empty is on the bar: the chip names the
column, *birthday 1981 – 1985*, and removing it brings the rows back.

**What would reverse it:** clients of the page that each apply the clause rule themselves, as
`GraphClient` does, skipping a clause they cannot answer. Then a column clause could go to the page
as itself. Mosaic's clients apply every clause they are handed, so the rule lives in the clause. And
for the greying: readers who take a greyed type with no time as one the window judged, often enough
that the chip does not correct them; then the types without the column are drawn apart from the
window's greyed-out vertices — fainter, or listed in the legend as having no time — and are still
not kept whole.

**What would reverse it:** a corpus whose times arrive as text with no datatype, such as one written
without a shape. Then a column is offered when its values parse, and the parse is the rule. And for
the setting: a page with two timelines over one graph, each on its own column. Then the column is a
prop of the part again, and the setting is the default the root hands it.

## Filtered is greyed out, and playing does not change that

**What the window leaves out is greyed out, and nothing is hidden.** What has no time is left out
too. Cosmograph 2.5 does the same: it hands cosmos.gl `highlightedPointIndices` and
`highlightedLinkIndices`, and the rest takes the greyout opacity. So there is no "hide" preference for playback. A graph that grows over time reads as points
coming into colour against the shape of the whole.

**What would reverse it:** readers who take the greyed-out points as present while it plays, often
enough that the counts beside the canvas do not correct them. Then playback dims what is out of the
window further than a filter does, and still does not hide it, because a hidden point moves the
layout's sense of where things are.

## A date on a relation is a vertex

**A relation that carries a time, such as when a prize was won or since when two people know each
other, is a vertex between its two ends: the W3C's n-ary relations, schema.org's `Role`.** A Nobel
`Award` with its `year` stands between the laureate and the category, and LDBC's `knows` is a
`Knowing` with its `creationDate`. The timeline filters those vertices, and the edges into the
greyed ones grey with them by the rule that a link needs both its ends. Cosmograph's `useLinksData`,
a filter over the edges themselves, is not built. A fossil corpus has no edge properties, because a
shape cannot declare one before SHACL 1.2 and Turtle 1.2 are Recommendations; fossil plans them in
its own `design/later/edge-properties`.

**What would reverse it:** a corpus that writes edge properties. Then an edge table lists its columns
as a vertex table does, a link gets an index as a vertex does, and a clause over an edge column keeps
the edges it matches and their endpoints — Cosmograph's rule, `endpointPointsOf`.

## What a test reads

The part is accessible first, in [the testing rule's](/docs/design/testing) order. Play and pause are
one button, *Play time* or *Pause time*, with `aria-pressed`, disabled and described with no range;
the chart is `aria-busy` while its query runs; the window the clause holds is a group whose
`aria-valuetext` reads *1950 – 1960*; and a pause or the end is a status, *Paused at 1950 – 1955*.
`ChartTimeline` registers its `x` scale under its title in `window.__KANZO_TESTING__.charts` when a
test has installed the hook. `TimelineHarness` then brushes in data and plays.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/charts/chart-timeline.test.tsx` ("reads a brushed range as the edges it
spans, in axis order — the frames play shows"; "has nothing to play without a range, as
Cosmograph's has not"; "is 50 ms a bar on 60 bars or more, and slower on fewer, so a sweep takes as
long"; "is 500 ms at least under prefers-reduced-motion"; "makes Play a bare glyph the band's height,
flush at its left and 4 px from the bars, as Cosmograph's"; a brushed window sticks to the nearest edges, at least one bar
