A timeline is a brushable histogram of one temporal column, which filters the page to the brushed
range and can play that range forward. `ChartTimeline` is the analytics part; `GraphTimeline` places
one under a graph canvas over the corpus's temporal columns. The reference is Cosmograph's
`CosmographTimeline`, read from `@cosmograph/ui` and `@cosmograph/cosmograph` 2.5.1. This page is
why ours is shaped as it is.

## A histogram whose brush owns one clause

**A timeline is two layers of bars over `bin(field)` and an interval brush that publishes one clause:
the bars behind are the column unfiltered, the bars in front are what the page keeps, the window
included, and the brush's clause is a chip in the bar like any other control's.** So the window is
in colour and what it leaves out is grey, as in Cosmograph. The brush sits on the bars behind and
exempts them alone (`peers: false`): they draw every bar for it to stick to, and the bars in front
are filtered by it. It is drawn as an outline, because d3's grey fill over the window read as the
part left out. Cosmograph's `Timeline` draws the same two
layers (`highlightSelectedData`) and publishes `isBetween(column, range)` from a client of its own.
Ours is a `ChartRoot` of `ChartRectY` marks and a `ChartBrushX`. The bins are SQL, through vgplot's
`bin()`, which bins a temporal channel by date.

**One clause means one source, for as long as the window lasts.** An interval is its clause's source,
and vgplot builds new interactors with every plot, so a plot rebuilt under a window left that window
in the selection with no brush to move or clear it, and the next brush published beside it: the
bar held two windows and intersected them. Two things rebuilt it. `bin()` returns a new closure per
call, which the plot's signature reads by identity, so every render was a new grammar: `bin` on
`/analytics` is made once per field and options. And a resize or a theme change rebuilds by design:
`ChartRoot` hands each interactor's value to the one that replaces it, which draws it and publishes
it as its own while the old clause is withdrawn — and withdraws it outright where the grammar
changed. *What would reverse it:* interactors in vgplot that outlive their plot, or a plot that
resizes without being rebuilt; then there is nothing to hand over.

**It replaced the dashboard's private `Timeline`**, the filter control behind a temporal field's chip
(`packages/ui/src/charts/dashboard-filters.tsx`), which drew the same marks with no playback and
could not be placed anywhere else. The chip renders `ChartTimeline` now: one implementation, as the
graph and the dashboard need the same part. **Cosmograph's binning was turned down**: it reads the
whole column into the browser and bins it in JavaScript, 250 bars by default. Ours asks the
coordinator for one row per bin, which a pre-aggregate can answer.

**What would reverse it:** a temporal column whose bins cannot be a `GROUP BY` — a time with a
timezone per row, read as wall-clock time. Then the bins are computed over the rows the client holds,
as Cosmograph's are.

## Playback moves the brush through its owner

**A clause belongs to the source that published it, and only that source changes it, so playback
asks the brush to move.** Each tick moves the interval interactor as its own `publish(extent)` does —
its `value`, its brush moved silently, and `selection.update(interval.clause(window))` — but with the
window in data rather than pixels. `publish` inverts a pixel floored to the interactor's
`pixelSize`, so a window it set would hold *1909.9* where the bar starts at *1910*. The brush moves,
and its clause, built by the interactor, moves with it.

**The window sticks to the bars' edges, Cosmograph's `stickySelection`.** When a drag ends — a
d3-brush `end` with a `sourceEvent`, which a move made in code never has — the window is moved to the
nearest edges of the bars the brushed mark drew, at least one bar wide. So it reads *1910 – 1940*,
its chip too, and a tick moves each end to the next edge: one bar, whatever its width, a month of
uneven days included. The edges are the mark's own `x1` and `x2`, the bins vgplot asked for, so
nothing is binned twice.
Mosaic draws this line itself. An interval interactor never follows its selection, while an input
that is driven from outside is bound to a `Param`: `Slider` follows a `Param`'s `value` events and
not a `Selection`'s, and `PanZoom` follows the `Param`s of its domains. Cosmograph's
`setSelection()` is the same shape, a method on the timeline rather than a write to the crossfilter.
`ChartTimeline` reaches its interactor through `Plot.interactors`, which vgplot exposes.

**Play sweeps the axis, as Cosmograph's timeline does.** With no window, it starts one a bar wide at
the start of the axis; with one, it moves that window, its width kept, from where it is. Every 50 ms
the window moves by one bar, and at the end of the axis it stops and the button is no longer
pressed. A pause leaves the window where it is. So Play is never disabled while there are bars: the
first version disabled it until a window was brushed, and a person who pressed it to see the graph
grow had first to learn to drag. There is no stop: a window is let go as any filter is, from its chip
or the page's **Clear**, and Cosmograph's stop is that and a pause.

**Under `prefers-reduced-motion` it still plays, a bar every 500 ms.** The rule on
[preferences](/docs/design/preferences) is to honour the query wherever something moves, and a sweep
repaints the whole canvas twenty times a second. It is not dropped, because the sweep is what the
person asked for and the pause is one press away; it is slowed tenfold, so each frame is a state to
read rather than a flicker. *What would reverse it:* a reader under the query for whom even that is
too much; then Play steps one bar per press.

**Play time is the timeline's first control, before the bars, and the window's range reads after
them.** It sat under the bars beside the readout, an icon identical to the canvas toolbar's layout
transport, so two play buttons a few centimetres apart did different things. It is now an outlined
button labelled *Play time* in words, with a tooltip, at the start of the bars as Cosmograph's is —
the toolbar's is a bare icon, so the label is what tells them apart, and a second icon would have
cost bytes to say less; the range, *1910 – 1940*, is right of the bars where the window ends.
*What would reverse it:* a timeline too narrow for a labelled button beside its bars, such as one in
a dashboard chip; then the label goes and the tooltip and the accessible name stay. A tick does not wait for the
graph: each one publishes, and the store keeps the newest answer and drops a stale one
(`packages/graph/src/core/store.ts`). A playing timeline therefore never draws behind its own clause.

**Two alternatives were turned down.**

- **`value` and `onValueChange` on the brush**, Ark's controlled pair. React would hold the range
  beside the clause that already holds it: two sources of truth for one filter.
- **Patching mosaic-plot so an interval follows its selection.** That moves the brush when another
  source updates the clause, and the model says no other source does. Clearing is the one case where
  something else touches the clause: the bar's chip and the page's **Clear** retract it, and the
  interactor's `reset` already answers that.

**What would reverse it:** an interval interactor in Mosaic bound to a `Param` — the brush following
a value, as `Slider` does. Then playback writes the `Param`, and `ChartTimeline` stops reaching into
`Plot.interactors`. A second one, for the tick: frames whose answers lag the window visibly on a
large corpus. Then the tick waits for the page's clients to answer before the next one.

## Time is read from the schema, never guessed

**A column is temporal when the corpus says it is: a `date` or `timestamp` column, or one whose
`datatype` is `xsd:date`, `xsd:dateTime` or `xsd:gYear`.** `fossil_columns.datatype` carries the IRI
the shape declared, so an `xsd:gYear` stored as an `int32` is drawn as years. Cosmograph instead
samples 100 values and calls a column of year-like integers a year. A count of rows, a price or an
id in that range would be read as a date, and the schema already says which column is a year.

**The column is a setting of the graph, not a selector of the part's.** `time-by` sits beside
*X axis* and *Group by* in `GRAPH_SECTION`, and the root answers its options from the corpus's
temporal columns, as it answers theirs. Cosmograph's own app configures its timeline the same way, in
its settings panel. A selector inside the part would have been a second door to a choice the graph
already makes in one place. None, the default, draws no timeline. The histogram is the union of the
tables that have the column, one colour, and while a window is set only the vertices with the
column inside it stay in colour.

**The window reaches the page as identity, not as its column.** A window is `date BETWEEN …`, and
only the types with a date can answer it: published straight into the page's crossfilter, it reached
every client of the page, and a dashboard's relation or a filter over a type with no date failed on
the column it does not have. So the timeline publishes into a crossfilter of its own, bridged to the
page by `semiJoinOf(key, timeline)` — `dense_id IN (SELECT dense_id FROM <the types with the
column> WHERE <the window>)` — the way a dashboard's tiles cross. It names the key alone, so every
client answers it, without a Binder error. The chip reads *date 1910 – 1940*: the bar names a
bridged clause by its parts, and does not prefix a part with its bridge's name when the part already
reads as it — the bridge is called by the column.

**A vertex without the column is outside every window, so it is greyed, as Cosmograph greys it.**
The bridge was first an anti-join, `dense_id NOT IN (… WHERE NOT <the window>)`, which dropped only
the dated rows the window leaves out and kept every type without the column whole. In keasy that read
wrong: on SNB, a window over 30 birthdays left *340,163 of 341,661* vertices in colour, every post,
place and tag among them, and it looked like a window that selected more than it should. Cosmograph's
`Timeline` filters its points by `isBetween(column, range)`, and a point with no time answers false,
so it is greyed with what the window leaves out. The semi-join is that rule over identity: only the
rows that have the column and fall in the window are kept, so the same window reads *30 of 341,661*.
The edges follow by the rule that a link needs both its ends.

**A dashboard over a relation whose root has no time column shows no rows while a window is set.**
It answers the same clause through its root, and none of its keys are in the window. That is Mosaic's
model: a client applies every clause it is handed, and a cross-filter is the rows every clause keeps,
so a relation that cannot be in the window is not shown as if it were. It is not left whole, which
would say the window had nothing to do with it. Why it is empty is on the bar: the chip names the
column, *birthday 1981 – 1985*, and removing it brings the rows back.

**What would reverse it:** clients of the page that each apply the clause rule themselves, as
`GraphClient` does, skipping a clause they cannot answer. Then a column clause could go to the page
as itself. Mosaic's clients apply every clause they are handed, so the rule lives in the clause. And
for the greying: readers who take a greyed type with no time as one the window judged, often enough
that the chip does not correct them; then the types without the column are drawn apart from the
window's greyed-out vertices — fainter, or listed in the legend as having no time — and are still
not kept whole.

**What would reverse it:** a corpus whose times arrive as text with no datatype, such as one written
without a shape. Then a column is offered when its values parse, and the parse is the rule. And for
the setting: a page with two timelines over one graph, each on its own column. Then the column is a
prop of the part again, and the setting is the default the root hands it.

## Filtered is greyed out, and playing does not change that

**What the window leaves out is greyed out, and nothing is hidden.** What has no time is left out
too. Cosmograph 2.5 does the same: it hands cosmos.gl `highlightedPointIndices` and
`highlightedLinkIndices`, and the rest takes the greyout opacity. So there is no "hide" preference for playback. A graph that grows over time reads as points
coming into colour against the shape of the whole.

**What would reverse it:** readers who take the greyed-out points as present while it plays, often
enough that the counts beside the canvas do not correct them. Then playback dims what is out of the
window further than a filter does, and still does not hide it, because a hidden point moves the
layout's sense of where things are.

## A date on a relation is a vertex

**A relation that carries a time, such as when a prize was won or since when two people know each
other, is a vertex between its two ends: the W3C's n-ary relations, schema.org's `Role`.** A Nobel
`Award` with its `year` stands between the laureate and the category, and LDBC's `knows` is a
`Knowing` with its `creationDate`. The timeline filters those vertices, and the edges into the
greyed ones grey with them by the rule that a link needs both its ends. Cosmograph's `useLinksData`,
a filter over the edges themselves, is not built. A fossil corpus has no edge properties, because a
shape cannot declare one before SHACL 1.2 and Turtle 1.2 are Recommendations; fossil plans them in
its own `design/later/edge-properties`.

**What would reverse it:** a corpus that writes edge properties. Then an edge table lists its columns
as a vertex table does, a link gets an index as a vertex does, and a clause over an edge column keeps
the edges it matches and their endpoints — Cosmograph's rule, `endpointPointsOf`.

## What a test reads

The part is accessible first, in [the testing rule's](/docs/design/testing) order. Play and pause are
one button, *Play time* or *Pause time*, with `aria-pressed`, the chart is `aria-busy` while its query runs, and the brushed range
is a group whose `aria-valuetext` reads *1950 – 1960*. `ChartTimeline` registers its `x` scale under
its title in `window.__KANZO_TESTING__.charts` when a test has installed the hook. `TimelineHarness`
then brushes in data and plays.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/charts/chart-timeline.test.tsx` (a tick moves the window one bar, in axis
order, and stops at the end of the axis; "starts a window one bar wide at the start of the axis when
there is none, as Cosmograph's does"; "puts Play time first, before the bars, and the window's range
after them"; a brushed window
sticks to the nearest edges, at least one bar wide; a year reads as its integer, on the axis too; the
figure, its button and its group are what a harness reads),
`packages/ui/src/charts/filter-bar.test.tsx` ("names a bridged part once when it already reads as its
bridge's name — a timeline's window"),
`packages/graph/src/parts/graph-timeline.test.tsx` (it draws nothing until `time-by` is chosen, and
nothing for a column the corpus lacks; "crosses the window into the page as a clause every client
answers, greying every vertex without the column"; "plays the whole axis with no window: one bar wide
from its start, to its end, then stops"; "plays a window from where it is, the same width, to the
end of the axis"; "pauses where it is, the window kept"; "holds one window, which each brush replaces — through a
re-render and a rebuild of the plot"; "keeps the window through a rebuild of the plot, drawn and
playable"; "draws the window's bars in front, and what it leaves out only behind"; "draws every tick
label whole, the first one at the axis's edge too"), `packages/mosaic/src/clause.test.ts` ("crosses clauses on some rows as a semi-join, which keeps
no row the table does not hold") and `packages/graph/src/parts/graph-section.test.tsx` (the
setting offers the `xsd:gYear` column by its datatype, and no integer of the same range).
