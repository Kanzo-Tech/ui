title: Findings
description: Why a finding is always one result and a group is many but both are one item with one action, why its place is the host's and reaches the library only through describe, why an item's message wraps on a line of its own, why the header is parts, and why the entry is a badge that opens a popover — and what would reverse each rule.
---

What a check found is drawn in two products here: Keasy's Discover validates a graph against SHACL
shapes and reports seventeen thousand results, and its recipe editor shows what fossil's compiler
found in a program. Both had their own rows, their own tally and their own popover. This page is why
they now share one model and one set of parts, and where the line between the library and the host
falls. [Findings](/docs/data-display/findings) is the reference.

## One result, or a group

**A `Finding` is always one result: a rule that failed at one place. A `FindingGroup` is many results
sharing a key — a rule, and usually a path — with a `count`, every place, and a `sample` to open.**
The two are different types, not one type with an optional count, because a model that might be one
or many is one whose every reader has to ask which — the item below asks once, so nothing else does. `groupFindings(findings, keyOf)` makes groups from
findings; `tallyFindings` counts both, a group by its `count`.

The references agree on the split and differ only on where it is made:

- **The W3C SHACL validation report** (`sh:ValidationReport`) is a flat list of `sh:ValidationResult`,
  each one focus node, one path, one value, one source shape and constraint component, one severity.
  That is our `Finding`, and SHACL's three severities — `sh:Violation`, `sh:Warning`, `sh:Info` — are
  our `FindingSeverity`.
- **TopBraid EDG and shacl-play** group that list by shape and then by constraint for a reader,
  because a report of seventeen thousand results read one by one is not read. That is our
  `FindingsGroup` heading, and a `FindingsItem` given a group.
- **Great Expectations** reports a failed expectation as counts and a sample of the unexpected
  values (`unexpected_count`, `partial_unexpected_list`), never the whole list. That is the group's
  `count` beside its `sample`, and why `count` is its own field: a host may hold a summary of
  seventeen thousand and the places of only some.

**The key is the host's.** Rule and path is the default reading of a SHACL report; a compiler might
group by code, a form by field. `groupFindings` orders worst first and keeps the host's order within
a severity, so a list sorted by line stays sorted by line.

**What would reverse it:** a consumer whose groups nest — shape, then path, then value — and who
needs the items to know it. Then a group holds groups, and a group's item opens onto items rather
than onto a sample.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/lib/findings.test.ts`, "keeps one group per key, counting every member and
sampling the first", "orders the groups worst first, and by first appearance within a severity" and
"counts a group by its count, not by its sample".

## The place is the host's

**A finding's `place` is a type parameter the library never reads. A view takes the host's
`describe(place)`, which returns where it is and what opening it shows: `{ where, detail? }`.** This
is VS Code's Problems panel: a diagnostic is a severity, a message, a code and a source over a
*location*, and the panel draws the location through the resource it belongs to, never by knowing
what is inside the file. The Language Server Protocol's `Diagnostic` is the same shape, with a
`Range` as its place.

So the library imports neither RDF nor LSP types. **The RDF place — a focus node, a path, a value — and
its description live in rudof-wasm's bindings, where the report is read; the code place — a line and a
column — lives in fossil's editor.** Each host writes one `describe`: *totalCost · Project/123*;
*Line 12*.

**What would reverse it:** two hosts writing the same `describe` for the same kind of place. Then that
reading moves into a package both depend on — still not this one, unless the place is a type this
library already owns.

## One item, one action

**One result and a group are one part, `FindingsItem`, given either: the group wears its count on the
severity badge and opens onto its sample. Each item has at most one action — *Go to field*, *Go to
line*, *Show 362* — and it is the item's `action`, not the place's. A sampled place is read, never
acted on.** VS Code's Problems panel is the reference: grouped, and one thing to do per entry, which is
to go there. The metadata-form showcase's popover arrived at the same shape on its own — severity,
where, *Go to field*, the message — and it was the clean one of the three call sites.

**It replaced** `FindingRow` and `FindingGroupRow`. They drew the same two lines from the same
`Diagnostic` parts and differed in what the data is, which the item can see for itself (`count` is
only on a group). Their action lived in two places — `describe(place).action` on one, a prop on the
other — and the group's sample drew a button per place, so an opened group of three was four buttons
saying *Show*. In Discover that sat beside a rule's own *Show all 44 violations*, written by the host
as a stray `<li>` in the group because the heading had no place for it: three ways to put nodes on the
page from one popover. Now a group's heading takes one `action`, an item takes one, and a place takes
none. The name is Ark's grammar, base and part, as `AccordionItem` is an entry of an accordion.

**An action may say whether it is on.** `pressed` makes it Ark's `Toggle`: Discover's *Show* puts
nodes on the page and pressing it again takes them off, which the host was writing by hand with
`aria-pressed`. It is a field of the action rather than a second kind of action, because the one thing
the item owns about it — closing the popover before it runs — is the same either way.

**What line 1 reads** is one result's place as `describe` reads it, or a group's rule label; the item's
children replace it, which is how a group says what its members share — a path, *totalCost*. Children,
not a `where` prop, because it is markup.

**What would reverse it:** a host whose reader needs two things to do at one place, each as common as
the other — *Go to line* and *Quick fix*, say. Then an item takes actions as children, as
`DiagnosticActions` does, and closing the popover becomes the part's to offer rather than the
action's to carry.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/composites/findings.test.tsx`, "is the same item with its count on the
badge, one action for all, and a sample to read rather than to act on", "is a region named by its
title, with the tally and its one action beside the name rather than in it" and "is a toggle when it
says whether it is on, and a plain button when it does not"; `packages/ui/src/index.test.ts`, the
tombstones `!FindingRow` and `!FindingGroupRow`.

## An item is two lines, and the message wraps

**Line 1 is the severity badge, where and the action; line 2 is the message, on a line of its own,
wrapping. Nothing is held to one line but the badge and the buttons.** It is how a compiler prints a
diagnostic, and it is the row metadata-form's `IssueDiagnostic` arrived at after the one-line header
measured a message 0 px wide in a panel a fifth of the workspace wide (`DiagnosticHeader`'s comment
has the figures). `where` breaks anywhere, because an IRI has no spaces to break at.

An item is built on `Diagnostic`'s parts and adds nothing to them but the arrangement:
`DiagnosticTitle` truncates, which is right for a title beside other things, and the message is given
`basis-full` and `whitespace-normal!` because a line of its own has nothing to align with.

**What would reverse it:** a list where scanning many items at a glance is worth more than reading
any one — a log. Then the message truncates and the item opens to read it, as `DiagnosticTitle` does.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/composites/findings.test.tsx`, "puts the message on a line of its own that
wraps, and holds nothing to one line but the badge and the buttons".

## The header is parts

**`FindingsHeader` is the popover's header, and what goes in it is children: `FindingsSource` for
what was checked, `FindingsActions` for its controls, `PopoverDescription` for what the check
covered. Its title is not a part — it is the labels' *Findings*.** It replaced `FindingsContent`'s
`header` prop, which took markup as an attribute (the rule is on
[Conventions](/docs/conventions#structure-and-props)), and each host filled it differently: Discover
with a hand-built file row, scope line and three buttons, the editor with `PopoverHeader`'s title and
description, metadata-form with a description alone. The three now say the same four things in the
same places — *Findings*, `flights-shapes.ttl · 4 rules`, *↻ ⋯ Keep what conforms*, *1,204 of 3,310
nodes checked* — and differ only in the words.

It is laid out as an item is: a row that wraps, the title and the source growing on line 1, the
actions at its end, the scope on a line of its own. The minimal set came from asking, part by part,
whether something already draws it:

- **The scope is `PopoverDescription`**, not a part of ours. It is Ark's description, so it describes
  the dialog to a screen reader as well; a `FindingsScope` would have been the same element under a
  second name.
- **The title is `PopoverTitle`**, drawn by the header from `labels.title`, not a part a host
  places. Every call site said *Findings* or wanted to, the dialog is named by it, and a host that
  calls it something else says so where it says *Violation*: in the labels.
- **`FindingsSource` is new** because nothing draws a muted monospace label that grows and wraps
  anywhere except the item's own *where*, and it is that class, shared. `DiagnosticSource` is
  `nowrap` and hidden below `sm`, which is right beside a message and wrong for a file name.
- **`FindingsActions` is `DiagnosticActions` pushed to the end of the line**, under its own slot,
  and a group's heading uses it for its one action too. Icon buttons, and at most one text toggle:
  line 1 is meant to hold in a 28rem popover beside a file name, and words are what widen it.

**`FindingsContent` is only the popover now, and `FindingsList` the scrolling list.** Once the header
is a child it cannot be told from the list without reading children's types, so the list became its
own part — the vertical area and the switch to `empty`. **`empty` stays a prop**: it is the list's
fallback, drawn instead of the children and never beside them, as `Show`'s `fallback` is. The rule
against markup in a prop is about regions a caller cannot reorder or wrap, and a fallback has nothing
to be ordered against.

**What would reverse it:** a host that needs the title to be something other than words — an icon, a
link to the rules — often enough that labels cannot say it. Then the title is a part, as the source is.
And for `empty`: a host that needs the empty state *beside* a partial list. Then it is a part.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/composites/findings.test.tsx`, "heads the list with the title, what was
checked, its controls and what the check covered".

## The list scrolls down, never across

**The popover's list is a `ScrollArea` with `orientation="vertical"`.** Ark gives its content
`min-width: fit-content`, so one unbroken line set the list's width: rows ran past a 448 px popover to
507 px behind a horizontal scrollbar (measured 2026-10-02 on the findings example), and the first
`FindingsContent` dropped `ScrollArea` for a plain `div` to get away from it. Vertical sets the
content's floor to 0, hides the sideways overflow and draws no horizontal scrollbar. It is an option
and not the default, because a code block in a dialog relies on the sideways scroll.

**What would reverse it:** every `ScrollArea` in the library and its hosts passing `vertical`. Then
it is the default and `both` is the option.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/simples/scroll-area.test.tsx`, "vertical drops Ark's fit-content floor and
the horizontal scrollbar", and `packages/ui/src/composites/findings.test.tsx`, "lists in a
vertical-only area, so a long line cannot widen the popover".

## The entry is a badge that opens a popover

**In Discover and in the editor the findings are behind one badge that tallies them — *362 violations
· 17,364 warnings* — and opens a popover the host composes: a header (the rules file, the check's
controls and its scope), the grouped list, or an empty state.** The tally is what a reader needs at rest; the list is what
they need when they ask. A panel always open would spend a column on a list that is usually short or
empty, and a page of its own would take the reader away from the graph they are fixing.

**The badge is a button in every state.** The first `FindingsTrigger` stopped being one when nothing
was found, because there was no list to open. With a header and an empty slot there always is: *No
rules* opens onto choosing rules, *Valid* onto what was checked. `undefined` is *nothing checked* and
wears `outline`; a tally of zero is *clean* and wears `success`; otherwise the worst severity. Its
accessible name is its text, and its text is the host's: *362 violations* is a product's vocabulary and
a plural rule is a locale's.

**A group's heading carries its tally, not its paths.** A shape that failed on nine paths says *9*
and *2* in their families, and the paths are the rows beneath it.

**What would reverse it:** a host where the findings are the work rather than a report on it — a
data-quality console. Then the list is a page, the groups are its sections, and the badge is a
summary on another page.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/composites/findings.test.tsx`, "is a button named by the host's tally in
every state, painted by the worst" and "shows the empty slot, and not the list, when nothing was
found or nothing was checked".
