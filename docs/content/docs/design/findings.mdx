title: Findings
description: Why a finding is always one result and a group is many, why its place is the host's and reaches the library only through describe, why a row's message wraps on a line of its own, and why the entry is a badge that opens a popover — and what would reverse each rule.
---

What a check found is drawn in two products here: Keasy's Discover validates a graph against SHACL
shapes and reports seventeen thousand results, and its recipe editor shows what fossil's compiler
found in a program. Both had their own rows, their own tally and their own popover. This page is why
they now share one model and one set of rows, and where the line between the library and the host
falls. [Findings](/docs/data-display/findings) is the reference.

## One result, or a group

**A `Finding` is always one result: a rule that failed at one place. A `FindingGroup` is many results
sharing a key — a rule, and usually a path — with a `count`, every place, and a `sample` to open.**
The two are different types, not one type with an optional count, because a row that might be one or
many is a row whose every action has to ask which. `groupFindings(findings, keyOf)` makes groups from
findings; `tallyFindings` counts both, a group by its `count`.

The references agree on the split and differ only on where it is made:

- **The W3C SHACL validation report** (`sh:ValidationReport`) is a flat list of `sh:ValidationResult`,
  each one focus node, one path, one value, one source shape and constraint component, one severity.
  That is our `Finding`, and SHACL's three severities — `sh:Violation`, `sh:Warning`, `sh:Info` — are
  our `FindingSeverity`.
- **TopBraid EDG and shacl-play** group that list by shape and then by constraint for a reader,
  because a report of seventeen thousand results read one by one is not read. That is our
  `FindingsGroup` heading and our `FindingGroupRow`.
- **Great Expectations** reports a failed expectation as counts and a sample of the unexpected
  values (`unexpected_count`, `partial_unexpected_list`), never the whole list. That is the group's
  `count` beside its `sample`, and why `count` is its own field: a host may hold a summary of
  seventeen thousand and the places of only some.

**The key is the host's.** Rule and path is the default reading of a SHACL report; a compiler might
group by code, a form by field. `groupFindings` orders worst first and keeps the host's order within
a severity, so a list sorted by line stays sorted by line.

**What would reverse it:** a consumer whose groups nest — shape, then path, then value — and who
needs the rows to know it. Then a group holds groups, and `FindingGroupRow` opens onto rows rather
than onto a sample.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/lib/findings.test.ts`, "keeps one group per key, counting every member and
sampling the first", "orders the groups worst first, and by first appearance within a severity" and
"counts a group by its count, not by its sample".

## The place is the host's

**A finding's `place` is a type parameter the library never reads. A view takes the host's
`describe(place)`, which returns where it is, what to do there and what opening it shows:
`{ where, action?, detail? }`.** This is VS Code's Problems panel: a diagnostic is a severity, a
message, a code and a source over a *location*, and the panel draws the location through the
resource it belongs to, never by knowing what is inside the file. The Language Server Protocol's
`Diagnostic` is the same shape, with a `Range` as its place.

So the library imports neither RDF nor LSP types. **The RDF place — a focus node, a path, a value — and
its description live in rudof-wasm's bindings, where the report is read; the code place — a line and a
column — lives in fossil's editor.** Each host writes one `describe`: *totalCost · Project/123* and
*Show*; *Line 12* and *Go to line*. Inside a popover, a row closes the popover before it runs the
action, so focus lands where the action sends it.

**It replaced** `FindingsGoTo` and the root's `onSelect`, which handed the whole finding back to one
callback: one action per list, chosen by the list's owner, and a row that could not say what it would
do. A place with no action draws no button — a control that goes nowhere is announced as one.

**What would reverse it:** two hosts writing the same `describe` for the same kind of place. Then that
reading moves into a package both depend on — still not this one, unless the place is a type this
library already owns.

## A row is two lines, and the message wraps

**Line 1 is the severity badge, where and the actions; line 2 is the message, on a line of its own,
wrapping. Nothing is held to one line but the badge and the buttons.** It is how a compiler prints a
diagnostic, and it is the row metadata-form's `IssueDiagnostic` arrived at after the one-line header
measured a message 0 px wide in a panel a fifth of the workspace wide (`DiagnosticHeader`'s comment
has the figures). `where` breaks anywhere, because an IRI has no spaces to break at.

The rows are built on `Diagnostic`'s parts and add nothing to them but the arrangement:
`DiagnosticTitle` truncates, which is right for a title beside other things, and the message is given
`basis-full` and `whitespace-normal!` because a line of its own has nothing to align with.

**What would reverse it:** a list where scanning many rows at a glance is worth more than reading any
one — a log. Then the message truncates and the row opens to read it, as `DiagnosticTitle` does.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/composites/findings.test.tsx`, "puts the message on a line of its own that
wraps, and holds nothing to one line but the badge and the buttons".

## The list scrolls down, never across

**The popover's list is a `ScrollArea` with `orientation="vertical"`.** Ark gives its content
`min-width: fit-content`, so one unbroken line set the list's width: rows ran past a 448 px popover to
507 px behind a horizontal scrollbar (measured 2026-10-02 on the findings example), and the first
`FindingsContent` dropped `ScrollArea` for a plain `div` to get away from it. Vertical sets the
content's floor to 0, hides the sideways overflow and draws no horizontal scrollbar. It is an option
and not the default, because a code block in a dialog relies on the sideways scroll.

**What would reverse it:** every `ScrollArea` in the library and its hosts passing `vertical`. Then
it is the default and `both` is the option.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/simples/scroll-area.test.tsx`, "vertical drops Ark's fit-content floor and
the horizontal scrollbar", and `packages/ui/src/composites/findings.test.tsx`, "lists in a
vertical-only area, so a long line cannot widen the popover".

## The entry is a badge that opens a popover

**In Discover and in the editor the findings are behind one badge that tallies them — *362 violations
· 17,364 warnings* — and opens a popover the host composes: a header (the rules file and *Change…*),
the grouped list, or an empty state.** The tally is what a reader needs at rest; the list is what
they need when they ask. A panel always open would spend a column on a list that is usually short or
empty, and a page of its own would take the reader away from the graph they are fixing.

**The badge is a button in every state.** The first `FindingsTrigger` stopped being one when nothing
was found, because there was no list to open. With a header and an empty slot there always is: *No
rules* opens onto choosing rules, *Valid* onto what was checked. `undefined` is *nothing checked* and
wears `outline`; a tally of zero is *clean* and wears `success`; otherwise the worst severity. Its
accessible name is its text, and its text is the host's: *362 violations* is a product's vocabulary and
a plural rule is a locale's.

**A group's heading carries its tally, not its paths.** A shape that failed on nine paths says *9*
and *2* in their families, and the paths are the rows beneath it.

**What would reverse it:** a host where the findings are the work rather than a report on it — a
data-quality console. Then the list is a page, the groups are its sections, and the badge is a
summary on another page.

*Held by* `packages/ui/src/composites/findings.test.tsx`, "is a button named by the host's tally in
every state, painted by the worst" and "shows the empty slot, and not the list, when nothing was
found or nothing was checked".
