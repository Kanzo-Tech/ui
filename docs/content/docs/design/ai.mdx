---

[Ask your data](/docs/ai/data) is what `@kanzo-tech/ai/data` ships. This page is why an answer is
shaped the way it is.

## An answer is a relation and a tile, and the tile is the whole question

<Callout type="warn">
**This section is a direction, and the half of it about the answer's shape is not built.** It was
decided on 13 October 2026. What is built is the read under the page's clauses and the engine's door,
below; what changes is what an answer *is*, which is what fixes *Add to the dashboard* losing the
question's filters.
</Callout>

**The model does not write SQL. It fills a spec whose every name is an enum the page generated from
the data it shows, and the answer that spec becomes is the dashboard's own vocabulary: a relation,
and a tile on it.** The tile carries the whole question — what is shown, its conditions (`where`) and
its ranking (`top`) — because [a dashboard's tile now carries its own](/docs/design/analytics#a-tile-carries-its-own-question).
So *Physics awards by university*, added to the dashboard, is still Physics awards by university,
where before a tile could hold neither condition nor ranking and the dashboard stored *awards by
university*.

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| `Answer` — `relation`, `where`, `show: Tile`, `top` | `Answer` — `relation` and `tile`, the tile holding `where` and `top` |
| `answerSchema(graph, relations)` and `checkAnswer`, the old answer tool's schema and validator | gone: Ask's schema is written from the linker's context, its fields paths ([Tasks](/docs/design/tasks#a-field-is-a-path-from-what-is-measured)), and its oracles are `JoinResolver`'s |
| `AnswerInput`, a relation by key; `AskSpec`, a subtraction from it | gone: `AskSpec` is the spec, `Answer` what it resolves to |
| `answerQuery`, a second compiler of a tile to SQL beside the dashboard's | gone: one compiler of a tile, the dashboard's, feeds both the chart and the rows |
| an answer drawn from a tool call the transport made up | an answer is a data part ([below](#an-answer-is-a-data-part-not-a-tool-call)) |

**It compiles with what the dashboard compiles with, and only that.** `relationQuery` makes the
relation, `JoinResolver.crossing` brings in a condition off its path, and the tile's compiler makes
the query — grouping, measure, order, `top`. The rows Ask reads and the marks the card draws come
from one function, so the sentence and the chart cannot describe two different queries.

**A page's clause reaches the answer as it reaches a tile.** A chip published on a relation is read
by the page's own resolver (`JoinResolver.crossing`): as it is on that relation, and on the answer's
through a semi-join on the key of a type both reach — a chip on *Physics*, published on
`LaureateAward>category>Category`, narrows `LaureateAward>university>University` by the prize, so the
University of California reads 16 and not its 43 in every category. It replaced matching a clause by
its columns alone, under which that answer kept none of its rows while the dashboard's tiles beside
it were narrowed: keasy's first evaluation pass showed a model that picked the right relation and an
answer that read nothing. A clause the answer's relation shares no type with keeps none of its rows,
as a tile's would (decided 11 October 2026), and is said, with the columns it names; the card prints
it under the tile, so an empty answer says why it is empty. A clause published on no relation is still
answered by column. What would reverse it is a resolver that crosses differently for tiles, and then
the answer follows it — the two never disagree.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/page-clauses.test.ts`, "crosses a chip on another relation by the key
both reach, as the page's tiles are crossed"; "keeps none of a relation's rows when it shares no type
with the chip's".

**The read goes through the engine's door, not the coordinator's queue.** The queue batches behind
`requestAnimationFrame` for the charts, and a hidden tab never fires one: a question asked before
switching tabs waited forever, and *Stop* only dropped the late answer. `Engine.query` takes the
chat's signal, so an abort ends the wait at once. An answer is one statement, which the queue had
nothing to consolidate with. What would reverse it is a coordinator whose queue takes a signal and
does not wait for a frame: then Ask goes back to reading where the charts read.

Under [the read door](/docs/design/reads) it stays the engine's: the query function is handed the
query's signal, and hands it to `Engine.query`; the cache keys the read by its statement and the
page's clauses, so a card redrawn after a remount reads nothing again.

**The card is a dashboard tile, and it takes the answer.** `AnswerCard` takes `answer` — what the
data part holds — not an AI SDK tool part, so an answer can be drawn outside a transcript. It draws
the dashboard's `ChartCard`, `DashboardStat` or `DetailTable` over the tile, with two verbs that
fall out of the shape: **Filter the page to these rows** publishes the tile's clause, built by
`JoinResolver.crossing` as every other crossing is — the third semi-join written by hand goes — and
**Add to the dashboard** puts the tile, its conditions and its ranking with it, in the relation's
spec. A kept answer is names, not SQL, so a restored transcript draws it again over the host's
graph, and nothing a transcript carried reaches the engine as a statement. It reads the fields'
facts the answer was asked over ([What a field is](/docs/design/fields)), never a second
`SUMMARIZE`.

**The references are the semantic layer's**: Looker's Conversational Analytics and ThoughtSpot Sage,
where a question is answered by picking fields of an explore or a worksheet, and Cube, LookML and
Malloy, where SQL is compiled from a model of the data rather than written against tables. A Looker
or Metabase tile is a saved query with its own filters, which is the tile this answer is.

**It replaced Databricks Genie's and Hex Magic's loop**: one SQL query per answer, written by the
model against DDL, and `gateStatement` between that text and the page's engine. The gate kept the
engine safe, but it could not make the model right. A local model shown
`"cac8fcfb-…"."Person"` wrote `"Person"` in one take and `"cac8fcfb-…"."scope"` in the next, and
both were refused. Making the gate tolerant guessed what the model meant, and showing plain names
moved the same mistake one step along. Every mistake of that kind is a name, and an enum removes
names from what the model writes. Genie and Hex run frontier models against warehouses whose
names are the reader's own. A page that runs whatever model its host points it at, over names a
corpus generated, cannot count on either.

**What it costs:** a question the spec cannot say, such as a window function, a ratio of two
aggregates or a join the `JoinGraph` does not have, gets no answer: the oracles refuse every spec
the model tries for it, and the reader is told so. There is
no SQL tool kept beside it as a fallback: two doors means two things to secure and two answers that
look different, and the spec is where the missing words get added.

**What would reverse it:** questions readers ask and the spec cannot say, recorded often enough that
growing the spec costs more than a second door. Then a SQL tool returns beside the spec, behind
`gateStatement` as it was — with `@kanzo-tech/mosaic`'s engine loading DuckDB's `json` again, whose
`json_serialize_sql` was the gate's parser — and the card says which one answered.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/answer-card.test.tsx`, "Add to the dashboard hands the
host its dashboards with the tile in the relation's spec, and where it landed".

## Whether a tile is on the dashboard is the host's spec, read

**`AnswerCard` takes the host's `Dashboards` and hands `onAdd` the whole next document, and *On the
dashboard* is the tile's id found in its relation's spec.** It is `Dashboard`'s own contract — a
value and the whole next value — and the controlled answer to a flag a card kept: that flag said
*added* the moment `onAdd` returned, before the host's save had succeeded, and a remount forgot it.
The tile goes on under the answer's id, the call's, which is what lets the spec be asked. A write the
host answers with a promise is waited on: pending until it settles, whatever the spec shows, and a
rejection drawn as a `Problem` under the tile, the thrown value whole.

`onAdd` is told the relation as well as its key, because a host's dashboard view is driven by a
`Relation` and a key cannot be read back into one.

**What would reverse it:** a host that keeps its dashboards in some shape other than `Dashboards`,
asking for the card. Then the card takes a predicate and a callback that build the next spec, and the
document is the host's again.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/answer-card.test.tsx`, "is on the dashboard while the host's spec
holds the tile, after a remount, and offered again once it is removed"; "is pending while the host's
write is, never on the dashboard before it settles, and draws a rejection as a Problem".

## An answer's verbs are on its tile, and Filter is there only when it filters

***Filter the page to these rows* and *Add to the dashboard* are icon buttons in the tile's own
header, beside its title, each named by `aria-label` and a tooltip; once the tile is on the
dashboard the second becomes *Open on the dashboard*, the host's `onOpen`.** Grafana's Explore adds
a query to a dashboard from the panel it is about, and its dialog ends in *Open dashboard*; Metabase
adds a question to a dashboard and then opens it there. The verbs used to be two text buttons above
the card, belonging to nothing on screen. A tile already has a place for its controls — the pencil
in `ChartCard`'s and `DashboardStat`'s header, in `CardAction` — so the verbs go there, through
`actions`, a node the host puts in the header before the pencil. Opening is the host's because the
view is: the card cannot switch it, so `onOpen` is told `{ relation, key, id }`, the add's own
answer and the tile's id. A host without it gets *On the dashboard*, disabled, rather than a verb
that does nothing.

`actions` sits beside `onEdit` rather than replacing it: the pencil's icon, label per kind and place
are the library's, and `Dashboard` is its only caller. *What would reverse it:* a host that wants a
pencil of its own; then `onEdit` goes and `Dashboard` passes its pencil through `actions`.

**Filter is disabled, and says *This answer keeps everything in view*, when its clause would keep
every row the page keeps.** In keasy's Ask over LDBC's social network, the model answered *browsers
used, as bars, over Person* with no conditions: the bars covered every browser, the clause kept every
person, and pressing Filter changed nothing a reader could see. The rule reads only what the card
already holds, so it costs no query: no conditions, a relation that is a type alone (a hop keeps only
the roots that have one), and bars over every category of their column — as many as the field's
`distinct`, none of them null, which the clause would drop. Disabled with its reason, not hidden, as
the timeline's Play is: the reader learns why the verb is not there. Its chip names what it filters,
each part read by `clauseLabel` as the filter bar reads a clause — *Ask: browserUsed · 3 selected*,
*Ask: Person.gender female* — where *Ask* alone said who had filtered and not to what.

**What it costs:** `distinct` is DuckDB's `approx_unique`, exact at the few dozen values a bar chart
answers, and it counts the whole relation. An answer read under a page that already narrowed its
column answers fewer categories than the field has, so its Filter stays enabled even when it would
keep exactly what the page keeps.

**What would reverse it:** readers pressing an enabled Filter that changes nothing, under a page that
had narrowed the column. Then the card counts the page's rows with and without the clause — one
query per answer — and disables on equal counts.

*Held by* `packages/ai/src/data/answer-card.test.tsx`, "draws its verbs in the tile's header, beside
its title"; "opens the tile on the dashboard once it is there, when the host says how"; "is disabled,
and says why, when its clause would keep everything in view"; "filters when the bars are ranked down
to some categories, or cross a hop"; "names the chip after what it filters, as the filter bar reads
each clause".

## Ask reads nothing to be offered its fields

<Callout type="warn">
**A direction, not built**: it waits on [What a field is](/docs/design/fields).
</Callout>

**Ask is offered paths from the types its linker keeps, and each path's facts come from the
`JoinGraph` — no query runs to build the prompt.** A field's kind, its role, its distinct count and
its commonest values are the corpus's own, carried on the graph; `answerRelationsOf` and
`readAnswerRelations` go, and with them the read that queued ahead of the dashboard's charts on the
page's one connection.

The measurement that made the types alone the offer still says why nothing should be read per
relation: measured 2026-10 with `readAnswerRelations` in DuckDB-WASM's Node build, over a graph
shaped like LDBC's social network benchmark — its 8 types, its 23 edges, its columns and its row
counts, the values synthesised:

| Scale | Offered | Relations | Queries | Read | Fields | Prompt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.1 (0.3 M vertices, 1.7 M edges) | the types alone | 8 | 16 | 0.4 s | 31 | 3,520 characters |
| 0.1 | and every hop | 54 | 290 | 9.4 s | 455 | 36,749 characters |
| 1 (3.2 M vertices, 17 M edges) | the types alone | 8 | 18 | 1.2 s | 31 | 3,581 characters |
| 1 | and every hop | 54 | 308 | 41.5 s | 455 | 39,018 characters |

Even the types alone cost a second at scale 1, every time a page opened Ask, for figures the corpus
had when it was written.

**What would reverse it:** a corpus without a profile as the common case — then the facts are read
once per corpus through [the read door](/docs/design/reads), still never per relation and never per
component.

## An answer's rows are on the page, as a table

**`AnswerCard` holds the rows the answer was read from as a visually hidden table, captioned *The
rows the answer was read from*.** A chart's long description is its data, best structured as a
table on the same page — the W3C WAI Images Tutorial's [*Complex
Images*](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/) — and Highcharts' accessibility module
keeps such a table for screen readers in the same way. The rows are the read's as the engine
returned them under the page's filter when the call ran, capped as the model's are; the tile above
follows the page afterwards and may no longer show them, which the caption's words make plain.

The same table is what a test proves an answer by: `AnswerHarness.rows()` reads it by its caption,
cell by cell. A figure asserted there is the engine's; asserted in the chart it is only drawn, and in
the reply it is only the model's quotation of it — on a replayed take, a recording's.

## An answer is a data part, not a tool call

<Callout type="warn">**A direction, not built.** Decided 13 October 2026.</Callout>

**Ask's transport writes the answer as a `data-answer` part of the assistant's message, and a
decision it needs from the person as a `data-ask` part; it writes no tool call.** No model called a
tool: the Task's spec was generated with `Output.object` and checked by oracles, and the transport
then wrote a `tool-input-available` *as the model would have written it*, so that `Chat`'s `tools`
renderer and an `AnswerCard` taking a `ToolPart` could draw it. A data part is the AI SDK's form for
exactly this — application data a stream writes beside the text, typed per name, reconciled by `id`
when it is written twice — and `Chat` draws it through a `data` renderer map beside `tools`.

The reference is the AI SDK's *Streaming Custom Data*: `createUIMessageStream`'s writer writes
`{ type: "data-answer", id, data }`, and a `UIMessage` typed with its data parts carries them to
`useChat`. An answer streamed in steps — the spec, then the rows — is one part written twice under
one `id`.

**What would reverse it:** a model that answers by calling a tool — a remote model with native tool
calling, given Ask as a tool. Then the call is real, and a tool part is what it is.

## A question to the person is the question part

<Callout type="warn">**A direction, not built.** Decided 13 October 2026.</Callout>

**When Ask must ask — an oracle's `Decision`, options and never free text — it writes the decision
as a `data-ask` part, the transcript draws it with `@kanzo-tech/ui`'s question part, and the
person's pick is sent back as the option's id in a data part of the reply.** Today the decision is
written as numbered text and the reply matched back against *1*, the option's id or its label, which
is the second of two ways to ask the reader: keasy's assistant already draws its decisions with a
question component. There is one question part, in `@kanzo-tech/ui`, and
[Admission](/docs/design/admission#a-question-is-one-part-in-kanzo-techui) is why it lives there.

The reference is MCP's elicitation, which `Decision` already follows: a request with a schema of
options, answered by a structured reply, never parsed out of prose.

**What would reverse it:** a decision whose answer cannot be a choice — a value the person types,
such as a threshold. Then the question part takes an input, as Questionnaire's question already
does, and the reply carries the value; it is still not parsed out of text.

## A chat is kept in the AI SDK's shape

<Callout type="warn">**A direction, not built.** Decided 13 October 2026.</Callout>

**`@kanzo-tech/ai` keeps a chat as the AI SDK keeps one — the `UIMessage[]` of a chat id — and the
host gives two functions, `load(id)` and `save(id, messages)`, and may give a third, `keep`, which
decides what of a message is stored.** A chat hook given an `id` reads its messages through
[the read door](/docs/design/reads) before the first turn, validates them with the AI SDK's
`validateUIMessages` against the package's data-part schemas — `answer`, `ask` — and hands them to
`useChat({ id, messages })`; at the end of every turn it saves, one save at a time per id. `keep`
filters the parts — a host drops reasoning, or keeps the answer's spec and not its rows — and what
it keeps must still validate.

The reference is the AI SDK's *Chatbot Message Persistence*: a chat is its id and its
`UIMessage[]`, loaded into `useChat` and saved when a response finishes, validated on the way back
in because what was stored is untrusted input. Keasy wrote the same thing by hand around its
assistant — a store, a queue of saves, a restore — and it is the part of a guided chat that is not
the product's.

**A stored chat that does not validate is a failure, handed to the host whole**, as every other
([failure](/docs/design/failure)); the library never drops the messages it cannot read and opens an
empty chat in their place, which would lose a person's conversation without saying so.

**What would reverse it:** a host that stores something other than the messages — an event log, a
server-side thread — and rebuilds messages from it. Then `load` and `save` still have this shape,
and the conversion is the host's.

**Breaking**, in a consumer's words, for this page:

- `AnswerCard` takes `answer`, not `part`. Draw it from `Chat`'s `data` renderers under `answer`,
  not from `tools.answer`.
- `Answer` is `{ relation, tile }`; a condition and a ranking are the tile's `where` and `top`.
- `answerSchema`, `checkAnswer`, `AnswerInput`, `answerRelationsOf`, `readAnswerRelations`,
  `readAnswer`, `askTransport`, `link`, `askAlternatives` and `questionOf` are gone from
  `@kanzo-tech/ai/data`.
- `@kanzo-tech/ai`'s `Question` and its parts are gone; use `@kanzo-tech/ui`'s.
- `Chat`'s `proposals` take the Task's `Proposal`, and pressing one sends its value.
- A chat restored by `id` needs `load` and `save`; if you saved conversations yourself, delete that
  code and hand the hook your two functions.
